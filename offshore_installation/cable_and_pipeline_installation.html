Cable and Pipeline Installation

WarningIncomplete cost result

This page does not currently produce a complete cable- or TCP-installation CAPEX result. Cable lay tasks can be parameterized from ORBIT screening inputs, but burial-spread rates, survey productivity and rates, demobilisation, weather delay and exceptional scope remain unresolved. TCP additionally lacks a qualified product, vessel configuration, task times and matching spread rates.

A user-provided Van Oord cable-installation benchmark is materially higher than the earlier proxy calculation, but its contract boundary and price basis have not been independently evidenced in this repository. It remains a reconciliation item rather than a model input. Missing terms below return not parameterized, not zero.

1. How an Installation Campaign Works

Offshore installation converts supplied cable or pipe into a connected, protected and tested offshore asset. A typical cable campaign proceeds as follows:

mobilise the installation spread → load product at port → transit to site → survey or prepare the route → pull in the first end → lay the product → return to port and splice when required → pull in the second end → bury the route → inspect and survey the installation → test and complete the asset → demobilise

The activities are not always strictly sequential. Pull-ins occur around individual lay sections, not only after the complete route has been laid. An export-cable splice can interrupt laying when another product length is loaded. Burial and post-burial survey normally follow as separate campaigns in the reference method.

This page uses one time-and-rate framework for four installation subclasses:

  1. 66–132 kV inter-array cables;
  2. 320–525 kV HVDC export systems;
  3. spoolable thermoplastic-composite-pipe (TCP) hydrogen infield pipelines; and
  4. spoolable TCP hydrogen export pipelines.

The accounting framework is shared. Product configuration, task times, installation rates and feasibility limits remain subclass-specific.

Cost boundary

The installation boundary begins when the contracted spreads mobilise and ends after the offshore testing included in their installation scope.

Scope Treatment
Cable or pipe manufacture and supply accessories Excluded; owned by the relevant cable or pipeline page
Lay-spread mobilisation, loading, transit, laying, pull-ins, offshore splices, lay-spread testing and demobilisation Included through lay-spread time
Burial-spread mobilisation, transit, trenching or burial, positioning and demobilisation Included through burial-spread time
Independent ROV and survey campaigns or support Included through ROV/survey-spread time
Crew, fuel, routine workboats and tugs, and contractor overhead Included in the applicable spread day rate
Crossings, landfall, protection materials and exceptional civil work Added separately when outside the spread scope
Converter stations, substations, hydrogen compression, development, finance, contingency, OPEX and decommissioning Excluded; owned elsewhere in the model

Every vessel, activity and material is charged once. If an ROV is onboard the lay vessel and included in the lay rate, it is not also assigned a separate ROV spread. If burial is performed by an integrated lay-and-bury spread, the separate burial term is zero and an integrated rate replaces the reference split.

2. Three-Spread Installation Cost Model

The reference calculation separates installation into laying, burial and ROV/survey spreads. Each spread produces an explicit number of chargeable days, which is later multiplied by its matching all-in day rate.

Spread Time output Rate input Cost contribution
Laying \(T_{\mathrm{lay}}\) \(r_{\mathrm{lay}}\) Lay-spread days multiplied by lay-spread rate
Burial \(T_{\mathrm{bury}}\) \(r_{\mathrm{bury}}\) Burial-spread days multiplied by burial-spread rate
ROV/survey \(T_{\mathrm{ROV}}\) \(r_{\mathrm{ROV}}\) ROV/survey-spread days multiplied by ROV/survey-spread rate

The three times are spread-days, not sequential project days. A lay vessel and an ROV/survey vessel working on the same calendar day each incur one chargeable day. Their costs therefore add even though their activities are concurrent.

For cable subclasses, ORBIT is the preferred source of task durations. The equations on this page define the required ORBIT configuration, map its task outputs to the three spreads and provide a transparent fallback when only the published task inputs are available. The same task is not estimated independently in ORBIT and again on this page.

Day-rate boundary

Rate Required inclusions Exclusions retained elsewhere
\(r_{\mathrm{lay}}\) Lay vessel, cable or pipe handling and lay equipment, crew, fuel, routine workboats and tugs, and contractor overhead Product supply, landfall civil work, crossings and protection materials
\(r_{\mathrm{bury}}\) Burial vessel, selected trenching or burial tool, crew, fuel, routine workboats and tugs, and contractor overhead Rock, mattresses, crossing structures and other protection materials
\(r_{\mathrm{ROV}}\) ROV/survey vessel, ROV and survey equipment, crew, fuel, routine workboats and tugs, and contractor overhead Specialist investigation or exceptional intervention packages

Mobilisation and demobilisation are represented as chargeable spread days. A quotation that prices them as lump sums must first be converted into an equivalent time-and-rate treatment or recorded as a separate fixed cost with a clearly non-overlapping boundary.

The model assumes full day-rate charging during weather standby. If a contract uses reduced standby rates, the weather-cost treatment must be changed rather than represented only by a time uplift.

The ORBIT cable-vessel file describes an operating-vessel rate with crew, but does not demonstrate the complete commercial boundary required above. For cable-lay screening only, this page treats the normalized cable-vessel proxy as a complete lay-spread rate including crew, fuel, routine workboats and tugs, and contractor overhead. It must be replaced together with its stated boundary for a commercial estimate. The same rate is not assigned to burial. The cable-lay screening calculation uses the executable lay value so that it can be reproduced from the pinned code revision. The burial value is retained as source context but is not adopted without a route soil, burial tool and matching spread.

3. Physical Quantities and Installation Configuration

ORBIT can estimate installation time only after the physical system and installation method have been configured correctly. Three length measures remain distinct.

Quantity Definition Model use
Route-kilometre Horizontal centreline distance between route endpoints Defines the corridor
Physical cable- or pipeline-kilometre Total product installed across every distinct asset Controls product mass, loading and supplied length
Pass-kilometre Distance travelled while one physical cable or pipeline is installed Controls lay and burial time; survey passes are counted separately

For asset type \(a\), physical length is:

\[ L_{\mathrm{physical},a} = N_{\mathrm{parallel},a}L_{\mathrm{route},a}(1+s_a) +L_{\mathrm{vert},a}. \]

Symbol Definition Unit
\(N_{\mathrm{parallel},a}\) Number of distinct physical cables or pipes count
\(L_{\mathrm{route},a}\) Horizontal route length route-km
\(s_a\) Installation slack fraction dimensionless
\(L_{\mathrm{vert},a}\) Total separately calculated vertical and structure allowance km
\(L_{\mathrm{physical},a}\) Total installed product length physical km

Slack is not added when project section lengths already include it.

ORBIT installs every array-cable section and export cable individually. The reference model therefore permits only one physical cable or pipeline in each lay or burial pass. For spread type \(x\):

\[ N_{\mathrm{pass},x,a} = N_{\mathrm{parallel},a}, \qquad L_{\mathrm{pass},x,a} = N_{\mathrm{pass},x,a}L_{\mathrm{route},a}. \]

For non-identical sections or routes, actual traversed lengths are summed:

\[ L_{\mathrm{pass},x} = \sum_m L_m. \]

Here \(x\) identifies the lay or burial spread, \(N_{\mathrm{pass},x,a}\) is its pass count and \(L_{\mathrm{pass},x,a}\) is pass-km. Survey length is calculated from its own required route passes because a survey can cover a corridor rather than every individual product.

TenneT calls its 2 GW export connection one cable system, but defines that system as four individual cables: positive pole, negative pole, metallic return and fibre-optic cable (TenneT, n.d.). Under the one-cable-per-pass ORBIT convention, one TenneT reference system therefore contains four physical lay units and four lay passes. Product length, mass and loading are calculated separately for each cable type; the indicative power-cable mass is not applied to the metallic return or fibre cable. This is a transparent screening convention, not evidence that TenneT’s contractor necessarily installs the four-cable bundle in four separate operations.

Lay-vessel feasibility and selection

Product linear mass affects two separate installation decisions. First, it determines how much product a given vessel can carry. Second, together with reel and handling requirements, it determines which vessel classes can perform the work. The second effect is material for TCP: a light spoolable pipeline can make a smaller multipurpose lay spread feasible, while a heavy cable may require a larger cable-lay vessel.

For candidate lay spread \(v\) and lay unit \(m\), the candidate-specific product length per load is:

\[ L_{\mathrm{load},m,v} = \min\left( \frac{M_{\mathrm{usable},v}}{\lambda_{\mathrm{product},m}}, L_{\mathrm{reel/partition},m,v}, L_{\mathrm{manufacturing},m}, L_{\mathrm{handling},m,v} \right). \]

A vessel is not qualified from payload alone. The product supplier and installation contractor must also verify minimum bend radius, tension, overboarding, dynamic loads, station keeping, water depth and route-specific operating limits. Those checks are retained at the highest defensible level as a qualification gate rather than reproduced through an unevidenced installation-engineering model:

\[ I_{\mathrm{qualified},m,v} = \begin{cases} 1, & \text{all product-, vessel- and route-specific checks pass},\\ 0, & \text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \]

The feasible candidate set is therefore:

\[ \mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{feasible},m} = \left\{ v\;\middle|\; L_{\mathrm{load},m,v}\ge L_{\mathrm{minimum},m} \quad\text{and}\quad I_{\mathrm{qualified},m,v}=1 \right\}. \]

The campaign-feasible set is \(\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{feasible}}=\bigcap_m\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{feasible},m}\) because the selected spread must qualify for every lay unit assigned to it.

Symbol Definition Unit
\(v\) Candidate lay spread or vessel class –
\(M_{\mathrm{usable},v}\) Usable product payload of candidate \(v\) after reel and deck equipment t
\(\lambda_{\mathrm{product},m}\) Linear mass of the selected product for lay unit \(m\) t/physical km
\(L_{\mathrm{reel/partition},m,v}\) Product length permitted by the candidate storage arrangement physical km/load
\(L_{\mathrm{manufacturing},m}\) Available continuous manufactured product length physical km/load
\(L_{\mathrm{handling},m,v}\) Product length permitted by the qualified handling and installation arrangement physical km/load
\(L_{\mathrm{minimum},m}\) Minimum continuous product length that must be carried between permitted field connections for lay unit \(m\) km
\(I_{\mathrm{qualified},m,v}\) Supplier- and contractor-confirmed installation qualification gate binary
\(\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{feasible},m}\) Candidate spreads that satisfy the explicit loading constraint and qualification gate set

Loading, reloads, transit, production, interface tasks and weather are then calculated separately for every feasible candidate, because vessel capacity and operating limits can change both time and cost. The cost-minimising qualified lay spread is:

\[ v^* = \underset{v\in\mathcal{V}_{\mathrm{feasible}}}{\arg\min} \left[ r_{\mathrm{lay},v}T_{\mathrm{lay},v} +C_{\mathrm{lay,fixed},v} \right]. \]

Here \(r_{\mathrm{lay},v}\) is the all-in day rate for candidate \(v\), \(T_{\mathrm{lay},v}\) is its chargeable campaign time and \(C_{\mathrm{lay,fixed},v}\) contains only explicitly quoted lay-spread costs not already represented by time and rate. Lower product mass can admit smaller candidates with lower rates, but the selected spread still depends on load count, productivity, mobilisation, operability and commercial boundary. If candidate-specific TCP rates are unavailable, the model reports the feasible vessel classes as alternative outcomes; it does not select \(v^*\) or calculate TCP installation CAPEX.

4. Lay-Spread Time

The lay-spread calculation follows ORBIT’s cable-installation task logic. ORBIT explicitly represents mobilisation, product loading, vessel storage, port returns, route laying, positioning, pull-ins, offshore splicing, termination/testing and transit (National Laboratory of the Rockies, n.d.). Demobilisation remains a separate unresolved input because the reviewed cable module does not contain a general demobilisation task.

Chargeable lay time is divided into port or other non-weather-uplifted time and offshore weather-exposed time:

\[ T_{\mathrm{lay,base}} = T_{\mathrm{lay,nonweather}} +T_{\mathrm{lay,offshore,base}}. \]

Symbol Definition Unit
\(T_{\mathrm{lay,base}}\) Lay time before weather uplift day
\(T_{\mathrm{lay,nonweather}}\) Port and other defined lay time not exposed to the offshore weather uplift day
\(T_{\mathrm{lay,offshore,base}}\) Offshore-exposed lay time before weather uplift day

Weather is applied later, after all base tasks have been counted.

Product per load

For a selected vessel \(v^*\), \(L_{\mathrm{load},m}=L_{\mathrm{load},m,v^*}\) from the candidate calculation in Section 3. Before selection, load counts and campaign time are evaluated separately for every feasible candidate. This preserves the discrete trade-off between a smaller vessel with more loads and a larger vessel with a higher day rate.

Symbol Definition Unit
\(L_{\mathrm{load},m}\) Effective product length per load for lay unit \(m\) and the selected vessel physical km/load
\(L_{\mathrm{physical},m}\) Physical product length of lay unit \(m\) physical km
\(L_{m,g}\) Length of product \(m\) assigned to vessel load \(g\) physical km
\(N_{\mathrm{load},c}\) Port loads assigned to separately mobilised lay campaign \(c\) count
\(N_{\mathrm{load}}\), \(N_{\mathrm{reload}}\) Total port loads and subsequent reloads count

One vessel load can contain several complete array-cable sections or other compatible products. A valid loading plan assigns product to loads subject to:

\[ \sum_m \lambda_{\mathrm{product},m}L_{m,g} \le M_{\mathrm{usable},v}, \qquad 0\le L_{m,g}\le L_{\mathrm{load},m,v}. \]

Every physical section must remain complete unless the product and installation plan permit a field connection. Reel partitions, storage compatibility and installation order must therefore be checked in addition to total mass. This is a discrete packing problem; dividing total campaign mass by vessel payload gives only a lower bound when the detailed loading plan is unavailable.

The ORBIT reference carousel has a 13,000 t maximum mass. It defines one cable-vessel candidate rather than a universal installation capacity. For example, an indicative 65 t/km HVDC pole cable would be mass-limited to 200 physical km/load before reel, manufacturing and handling limits are applied. That result is not transferred to the metallic-return or fibre-optic cables.

Loads and reloads are counted by separately mobilised lay campaign \(c\):

\[ N_{\mathrm{load}}=\sum_c N_{\mathrm{load},c}, \qquad N_{\mathrm{reload}} = \sum_c\max(0,N_{\mathrm{load},c}-1). \]

Port loading time is the sum of the actual load operations:

\[ T_{\mathrm{port\ load}} = \sum_g t_{\mathrm{load},g}. \]

ORBIT uses six hours for both the initial load and each reload (National Laboratory of the Rockies, n.d.). This is product-loading time only. It does not include the return transit and repositioning needed for a reload.

Pass count and load count are independent. Four physical export-cable passes do not by themselves prove four port loads: the result depends on the qualified storage and loading plan. Conversely, one continuous cable can require several loads and offshore splices.

Port returns

For one-way distance \(D_{\mathrm{port}}\) between the work site and loading port, vessel transit speed \(v_{\mathrm{transit}}\) in km/h and repositioning time \(t_{\mathrm{reposition}}\) in days:

\[ T_{\mathrm{port\ return}} = \sum_c \max(0,N_{\mathrm{load},c}-1) \left( \frac{2D_{\mathrm{port}}}{24v_{\mathrm{transit}}} +t_{\mathrm{reposition}} \right). \]

The port loading itself remains in \(T_{\mathrm{port\ load}}\), so it is not included again in this expression.

The ORBIT reference cable vessel uses \(v_{\mathrm{transit}}=11.5\) km/h. Its reload sequence includes the outbound and return transits; onsite positioning associated with an offshore splice is counted in the splice task below and is not added as \(t_{\mathrm{reposition}}\).

Route laying

Lay production time is lay pass-km divided by the selected operating lay rate, summed over passes \(p\):

\[ T_{\mathrm{route\ lay}} = \sum_p \frac{L_{\mathrm{pass,lay},p}}{q_{\mathrm{lay},p}}. \]

The reviewed ORBIT version 1.3 implementation uses \(q_{\mathrm{lay}}=0.4\) km/h, equivalent to 9.6 km/day of continuous operation (National Laboratory of the Rockies, n.d.). This is an operating rate, not an achieved weather-inclusive campaign rate. Product handling, stops, interfaces and weather are represented separately.

Pull-ins, splicing and testing

The model preserves the number of physical interfaces and splices because they create material step changes in vessel time. It does not require every minor handling action as a separate model input. Instead, a sourced aggregate duration is assigned to each event type \(e\):

\[ T_{\mathrm{event}} = \sum_e\frac{N_e t_e}{24}. \]

Event type \(e\) Aggregate duration \(t_e\) Included ORBIT tasks Counting rule
Array-cable section 27 h/section Positioning, preparation, two pull-ins, two termination/test tasks and lowering Once per physical array-cable section
Export power-cable interface sequence 26 h/cable Landfall and offshore-end positioning, preparation, pull-in, termination/test and lowering Once per qualifying export power cable; metallic-return and fibre tasks require matching inputs
Offshore splice 51.5 h/splice Positioning, cable recovery, splicing and lowering Once per required continuous-route load break

The aggregate durations are derived from the executable ORBIT defaults listed in Section 2 (National Laboratory of the Rockies, n.d.). They apply only while the lay spread remains occupied. Retaining the underlying task values in the source-input table preserves traceability, while the calculation uses the event totals that materially determine campaign duration.

For array cables, a reload normally supplies additional discrete cable sections and does not automatically create an offshore splice. For a continuous export route, exceeding the available continuous product length can require both a reload and an inline splice.

Lay-time aggregation

Non-weather-uplifted lay time is:

\[ T_{\mathrm{lay,nonweather}} = T_{\mathrm{mob,lay}} +T_{\mathrm{port\ load}} +T_{\mathrm{demob,lay}}. \]

Base offshore-exposed lay time is:

\[ \begin{aligned} T_{\mathrm{lay,offshore,base}} ={}& T_{\mathrm{site\ transit}} +T_{\mathrm{route\ lay}} +T_{\mathrm{port\ return}}\\ &+T_{\mathrm{event}} \end{aligned} \]

All terms in the two aggregation equations are chargeable days. \(T_{\mathrm{mob,lay}}\) is 1.5 full-rate-equivalent days in the cable screening case, representing ORBIT’s three calendar days at 50% of the day rate. \(T_{\mathrm{demob,lay}}\) remains unresolved. \(T_{\mathrm{site\ transit}}\) is initial offshore transit. Every event is assigned once.

5. Burial-Spread Time

The reference method uses a separate burial spread. Its task sequence is mobilisation, transit, tool deployment and setup, burial or pre-trenching passes, repositioning, tool recovery and demobilisation.

Non-weather-uplifted burial time is mobilisation and demobilisation:

\[ T_{\mathrm{bury,nonweather}} = T_{\mathrm{mob,bury}} +T_{\mathrm{demob,bury}}. \]

Base offshore-exposed burial time is:

\[ \begin{aligned} T_{\mathrm{bury,offshore,base}} ={}& T_{\mathrm{transit,bury}} +T_{\mathrm{setup,bury}}\\ &+\sum_p \frac{L_{\mathrm{pass,bury},p}}{q_{\mathrm{bury},p}} +T_{\mathrm{reposition,bury}}\\ &+T_{\mathrm{recovery,bury}}. \end{aligned} \]

Therefore:

\[ T_{\mathrm{bury,base}} = T_{\mathrm{bury,nonweather}} +T_{\mathrm{bury,offshore,base}}. \]

Symbol Definition Unit
\(T_{\mathrm{bury,base}}\) Total burial time before weather uplift day
\(T_{\mathrm{bury,nonweather}}\) Fixed burial-spread time outside the offshore weather uplift day
\(T_{\mathrm{bury,offshore,base}}\) Offshore-exposed burial time before weather uplift day
\(T_{\mathrm{mob,bury}}\), \(T_{\mathrm{demob,bury}}\) Burial-spread mobilisation and demobilisation day
\(T_{\mathrm{transit,bury}}\) Burial-spread offshore transit day
\(L_{\mathrm{pass,bury},p}\) Burial or trenching pass length for pass \(p\) pass-km
\(q_{\mathrm{bury},p}\) Operating burial or pre-trenching rate for pass \(p\) km/day/pass
\(T_{\mathrm{setup,bury}}\) Tool deployment and setup time day
\(T_{\mathrm{reposition,bury}}\) Positioning between burial passes or sections day
\(T_{\mathrm{recovery,bury}}\) Tool recovery time day

The reviewed ORBIT version 1.3 implementation contains \(q_{\mathrm{bury}}=0.4\) km/h and \(q_{\mathrm{pretrench}}=0.1\) km/h (National Laboratory of the Rockies, n.d.). These are reference defaults, not adopted base-case inputs. Route-specific soil, burial depth, tool selection, mobilisation, transit, deployment, recovery, demobilisation and the matching spread rate are required before burial time and cost are parameterized.

Burial pass length is not assumed equal to lay pass length. Sections near structures, landfall or designed protection zones may have different burial lengths, but only one physical cable or pipeline is buried in each modeled pass.

Rock, mattresses and crossing structures are excluded from burial-spread time unless the selected burial rate explicitly includes the corresponding installation method and materials. Their material and specialist-package costs normally remain exceptional.

If laying and burial are physically integrated, this separate calculation is not used. The project instead requires one integrated duration and one integrated spread rate.

6. ROV and Survey-Spread Time

The ROV/survey spread can perform independent pre-lay, as-laid, post-burial or inspection campaigns and can support laying, burial or interfaces. Independent survey time is calculated from surveyed pass length and survey rate:

\[ T_{\mathrm{survey,independent,base}} = \sum_k \frac{L_{\mathrm{survey},k}}{q_{\mathrm{survey},k}} +T_{\mathrm{ROV,interface}}. \]

Symbol Definition Unit
\(T_{\mathrm{survey,independent,base}}\) Independent survey and interface time before weather uplift day
\(L_{\mathrm{survey},k}\) Route or pass length surveyed in activity \(k\) km
\(q_{\mathrm{survey},k}\) Operating survey rate for activity \(k\) km/day
\(T_{\mathrm{ROV,interface}}\) Independent ROV time at pull-ins, joints, crossings or other interfaces day

ORBIT’s cable module supplies no independent survey-pass count or survey production rate. Its generic support-vessel transit speed is not a proxy for survey productivity. Survey scope, achieved production, mobilisation and the matching complete-spread rate therefore remain site-specific or contractor inputs; until they are provided, \(T_{\mathrm{ROV}}\) and the ROV/survey cost term are not parameterized.

ROV support that accompanies another spread is based on that spread’s actual weather-adjusted offshore time:

\[ T_{\mathrm{ROV,support}} = \alpha_{\mathrm{lay}}T_{\mathrm{lay,offshore}} +\alpha_{\mathrm{bury}}T_{\mathrm{bury,offshore}}, \]

where \(\alpha_{\mathrm{lay}}\) and \(\alpha_{\mathrm{bury}}\) are the fractions of lay- and burial-spread offshore time requiring a separately charged ROV/survey spread. They lie between 0 and 1 and must be defined from the installation method.

The reference lay and burial campaigns do not overlap. If support windows do overlap, \(T_{\mathrm{ROV,support}}\) is the duration of their union rather than the arithmetic sum of overlapping days.

Total ROV/survey-spread time is:

\[ T_{\mathrm{ROV}} = T_{\mathrm{mob,ROV}} +T_{\mathrm{demob,ROV}} +T_{\mathrm{survey,independent}} +T_{\mathrm{ROV,support}}. \]

Here \(T_{\mathrm{survey,independent}}\) is weather-adjusted independent survey time, \(T_{\mathrm{ROV,support}}\) is concurrent support time, and \(T_{\mathrm{ROV}}\) is total chargeable ROV/survey spread-days. Mobilisation and demobilisation are inputs for the selected survey spread; ORBIT’s generic support-vessel mobilisation is not adopted without a demonstrated survey scope.

Concurrent support is a separate spread cost, not additional project elapsed time. If ROV and survey equipment are onboard and included in the lay or burial day rate, the corresponding \(\alpha\) is zero and those days are not charged again.

7. Weather and Total Installation CAPEX

Weather is represented by a transparent time uplift rather than an ORBIT discrete-event weather simulation. Port loading and other defined non-weather tasks are not uplifted.

The ORBIT duration used here must therefore exclude simulated weather delay. If a weather-inclusive ORBIT result is used instead, the corresponding uplift \(w\) is set to zero.

For laying:

\[ T_{\mathrm{lay}} = T_{\mathrm{lay,nonweather}} +(1+w_{\mathrm{lay}}) T_{\mathrm{lay,offshore,base}}. \]

The weather-adjusted lay offshore time used by ROV support is:

\[ T_{\mathrm{lay,offshore}} = (1+w_{\mathrm{lay}}) T_{\mathrm{lay,offshore,base}}. \]

For burial, the model separates any fixed non-weather time from offshore-exposed work:

\[ T_{\mathrm{bury}} = T_{\mathrm{bury,nonweather}} +(1+w_{\mathrm{bury}}) T_{\mathrm{bury,offshore,base}}. \]

The weather-adjusted burial offshore time used by ROV support is:

\[ T_{\mathrm{bury,offshore}} = (1+w_{\mathrm{bury}}) T_{\mathrm{bury,offshore,base}}. \]

For independent survey:

\[ T_{\mathrm{survey,independent}} = (1+w_{\mathrm{ROV}}) T_{\mathrm{survey,independent,base}}. \]

Symbol Definition Unit
\(T_{\mathrm{lay}}\) Total weather-adjusted chargeable lay-spread time day
\(T_{\mathrm{bury}}\) Total weather-adjusted chargeable burial-spread time day
\(T_{\mathrm{lay,offshore}}\) Weather-adjusted lay offshore time day
\(T_{\mathrm{bury,offshore}}\) Weather-adjusted burial offshore time day
\(T_{\mathrm{survey,independent}}\) Weather-adjusted independent ROV/survey time day
\(w_{\mathrm{lay}}\) Lay offshore-time uplift for weather fraction
\(w_{\mathrm{bury}}\) Burial offshore-time uplift for weather fraction
\(w_{\mathrm{ROV}}\) Independent ROV/survey-time uplift for weather fraction

No numerical weather uplift is adopted until an evidence basis is provided. Different spreads require different weather treatment because their operating limits differ. If weather evidence is unavailable, base exposed time can be reported, but weather-adjusted duration and installation CAPEX remain not parameterized.

Once the three chargeable spread times are known, installation CAPEX is:

\[ C_{\mathrm{installation}} = r_{\mathrm{lay}}T_{\mathrm{lay}} +r_{\mathrm{bury}}T_{\mathrm{bury}} +r_{\mathrm{ROV}}T_{\mathrm{ROV}} +C_{\mathrm{exceptional}}. \]

Symbol Definition Unit
\(C_{\mathrm{installation}}\) Total installation CAPEX EUR2025
\(T_{\mathrm{lay}}\) Weather-adjusted chargeable lay-spread time day
\(T_{\mathrm{bury}}\) Weather-adjusted chargeable burial-spread time day
\(T_{\mathrm{ROV}}\) Chargeable independent and supporting ROV/survey-spread time day
\(r_{\mathrm{lay}}\) All-in lay-spread rate EUR2025/day
\(r_{\mathrm{bury}}\) All-in burial-spread rate EUR2025/day
\(r_{\mathrm{ROV}}\) All-in ROV/survey-spread rate EUR2025/day
\(C_{\mathrm{exceptional}}\) Work and materials outside the three time-based spreads EUR2025

For a campaign selected from candidate spreads, \(r_{\mathrm{lay}}=r_{\mathrm{lay},v^*}\) and \(T_{\mathrm{lay}}=T_{\mathrm{lay},v^*}\). A missing candidate-specific TCP rate therefore blocks the numerical TCP lay-cost term; it is not replaced by the cable-vessel rate or set to zero.

Exceptional cost is:

\[ C_{\mathrm{exceptional}} = N_{\mathrm{cross}}C_{\mathrm{cross}} +L_{\mathrm{protection}}c_{\mathrm{protection}} +N_{\mathrm{landfall}}C_{\mathrm{landfall}} +C_{\mathrm{other}}. \]

Here \(N_{\mathrm{cross}}\) and \(N_{\mathrm{landfall}}\) are counts; \(C_{\mathrm{cross}}\) and \(C_{\mathrm{landfall}}\) are EUR2025 per item; \(L_{\mathrm{protection}}\) is protected route length in km; \(c_{\mathrm{protection}}\) is EUR2025/km; and \(C_{\mathrm{other}}\) is any other explicitly scoped exceptional cost in EUR2025.

Crossings, landfall, protection materials and exceptional civil work remain site-specific. A term is set to zero only when the matching spread quotation explicitly includes the same scope.

Constant-EUR2025 normalisation

Each spread rate and exceptional cost is escalated in its original currency before conversion to euros:

\[ C_{2025,\mathrm{EUR}} = C_{y,s} \frac{I_{s,k}(2025)}{I_{s,k}(y)} FX_{\mathrm{EUR}/s}(2025) G_{\mathrm{market}}. \]

Symbol Definition
\(C_{y,s}\) Source cost in currency \(s\) and price year \(y\)
\(I_{s,k}\) Selected index for cost type \(k\) in the source market
\(FX_{\mathrm{EUR}/s}\) Euros per unit of source currency
\(G_{\mathrm{market}}\) Separately stated geographic-market factor

ORBIT version 1.3 states its vessel-library costs in 2024 USD (National Laboratory of the Rockies, n.d.). The screening conversion uses annual-average U.S. CPI-U values of 313.689 for 2024 and 321.943 for 2025, followed by the ECB 2025 annual-average exchange rate of USD 1.1300 per EUR (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics 2026b, 2026a; European Central Bank 2026):

\[ F = \frac{321.943}{313.689}\frac{1}{1.1300} = 0.9082413\ \mathrm{EUR}_{2025}/\mathrm{USD}_{2024}. \]

The factor gives 219,041 EUR2025/day for ORBIT’s 241,171 USD/day cable vessel and 111,440 EUR2025/day for its 122,699 USD/day generic support vessel. The first value is adopted only as a provisional complete cable-lay-spread rate under the explicit inclusion boundary in Section 2. The burial and ROV/survey rates remain unresolved because ORBIT does not demonstrate that either vessel proxy represents those complete scopes.

8. Installation-Subclass Differences

The ORBIT task structure is directly applicable only to cable installation. The shared equations can describe TCP work, but cable operating rates and task times are not transferred to TCP.

Subclass Lay configuration Loading, pull-in and splice treatment Burial and ROV treatment
66–132 kV inter-array cable One static three-core section per lay pass Vessel loads multiple discrete sections; two pull-ins per section; a reload does not normally create an inline splice Burial pass count follows individual cable routes; survey and ROV scope follows the selected cable method
320–525 kV HVDC export One physical cable per pass. The TenneT 2 GW reference has four physical cables and therefore four modeled lay passes (TenneT, n.d.) Product-specific capacity controls reloads; continuous-route load breaks may create inline splices; pull-ins occur at landfall and offshore interfaces Each physical cable is buried separately in the screening model; landfall and protection remain separate
Spoolable TCP hydrogen infield One qualified TCP length per lay pass Reel exchange, J-tube pull-through, field end fitting, tie-in and testing require TCP-specific inputs Burial, stability and ROV requirements depend on qualified product and route design
Spoolable TCP hydrogen export Same physical accounting, subject to long-route feasibility Repeated reels and intermediate connectors can dominate time; no cable splice or pull-in defaults are used All production, burial, survey and support inputs require supplier and contractor evidence

Public 66 kV examples span approximately 20–50 t/km, so loading must use the selected cable rather than voltage alone (Thor Offshore Wind Farm Environmental Material, n.d.).

An indicative 2,500 mm² copper 525 kV submarine cable has a linear mass of approximately 65 t/km, but this is not a universal HVDC mass assumption and applies neither to TenneT’s metallic return nor to its fibre-optic cable (Prysmian Group, n.d.; TenneT, n.d.).

Manufacturer information supports horizontal TCP lay from a small multipurpose vessel and continuous lengths of approximately 3–6 km, depending on diameter and pressure (Strohm, n.d.-b). This is the physical basis for admitting a smaller candidate spread to the vessel-selection calculation. The installation-cost benefit does not come from product mass directly: it arises when the lighter product and its handling envelope make that smaller spread feasible and the matching all-in rate is lower than the rate for the qualifying cable-lay spread. These are generic product-family capabilities, not a qualified hydrogen design point. DNV-ST-F119 supplies the offshore TCP standard basis, while DNV’s hydrogen work identifies hydrogen-specific qualification gaps (DNV-ST-F119 2019; DNV, n.d.).

Until product and contractor qualification is available, the model reports two vessel-class outcomes rather than forcing one selection:

TCP outcome Qualification result Cost treatment
Small multipurpose-vessel subclass Payload and reel constraints pass and \(I_{\mathrm{qualified},m,v}=1\) for the small spread Use the matching small-spread task inputs and day rate when supplied; currently not parameterized
Specialist lay-vessel subclass The small spread fails payload, reel or qualification requirements Evaluate the next qualified vessel class with its own inputs and rate; currently not parameterized

An offshore TCP export calculation requires:

  1. a named hydrogen-qualified product and operating envelope;
  2. route-specific pressure, stability and installation design;
  3. continuous-length, reel and field-connection information;
  4. a qualified vessel and overboarding configuration;
  5. bend, tension, collapse and pull-through verification;
  6. laying, burial and survey production rates;
  7. interface and connector task times; and
  8. all-in lay, burial and ROV/survey day rates.

An 80–100 km export route is far longer than the public 3–6 km continuous-length range. Until these inputs are available, TCP installation remains not parameterized. ORBIT cable rates, steel-pipeline rates and conventional flexible-pipeline rates are not substituted.

Required assertions

  • Physical product length increases with every parallel asset.
  • Every modeled lay and burial pass contains exactly one physical cable or pipeline.
  • A lay spread is considered only after the explicit loading constraints pass and the supplier/contractor qualification gate equals one.
  • Product linear mass changes candidate-specific product per load and can change the feasible vessel set; it does not directly impose a day rate.
  • Each feasible candidate’s campaign time is calculated using that candidate’s capacity, task inputs and operating limits before spread selection.
  • One TenneT 2 GW reference system produces four physical cable passes; the system count is not substituted for the cable count.
  • Every load satisfies product-specific mass, storage, partition and handling limits; a total-mass ceiling is not treated as a proven packing plan.
  • The initial product load is counted once per separately mobilised lay campaign and every later load in that campaign creates one reload.
  • An array-cable reload does not automatically create an inline splice.
  • A continuous export-cable load break creates a splice only when required by the product and installation plan.
  • Port loading is not weather-uplifted.
  • Weather is applied once to each exposed spread time.
  • Concurrent ROV support is charged as ROV spread-days without extending project elapsed time.
  • Overlapping ROV support windows are counted once for the ROV spread.
  • ROV or survey equipment included in another spread rate is not charged separately.
  • Crew, fuel, routine workboats and tugs, and contractor overhead are not added outside the spread rates.
  • Exceptional scope included in a spread quotation is not added again.
  • Any missing material task time, qualification, weather input or matching spread rate returns not parameterized, not zero.
