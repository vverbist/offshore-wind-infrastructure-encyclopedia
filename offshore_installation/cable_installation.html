Export-Cable Installation Reference

The NREL Atlantic Offshore Wind Transmission Study separates material and installation for a 525 kV, 2 GW HVDC bipole (Brinkman et al. 2024). The values below use the ECB 2025 annual-average exchange rate of 1 EUR = 1.1300 USD (European Central Bank 2026).

NREL component Converted route cost Cost owner
Cable material EUR 2.51 million/km HVDC Export Cables
Cable installation EUR 1.48 million/km This page
Material + installation EUR 4.00 million/km Shared perspective only

The conversion changes currency only. The NREL price year, route conditions, landfall scope and compatibility with TenneT’s dedicated metallic-return design must still be confirmed before using it as a constant-2026-euro model input.

ImportantSignificant installation cost favours higher-capacity assets

In the NREL reference, installation is about EUR 1.48 million/km compared with EUR 2.51 million/km for cable material. Much of the installation cost is driven by the route, mobilisation, vessel campaign, burial, crossings and pull-ins rather than increasing directly with transmitted capacity. This creates an economic tendency to use higher-capacity cables—and similarly higher-capacity pipelines—because the route-installation cost is spread over more transported power or product.

That tendency is not unlimited. Cable mass, handling limits, manufacturing availability, electrical losses, redundancy requirements and the consequence of losing one large asset can favour multiple smaller systems.

Installed-System Cross-Checks

Adriatic Link

Prysmian’s 2023 Adriatic Link award is approximately EUR 630 million for design, supply, installation and commissioning of a 1 GW bipolar link with about 210 km subsea and 40 km underground route (Prysmian Group 2023). This is EUR 2.52 million/km across the mixed route.

Applying the DEA underground-to-subsea cost ratio and the report’s 2023-to-2026 restatement factor gives an inferred 2026 subsea rate of EUR 2.60 million/km.

This remains a complete installed-system inference. The contract does not disclose its material/installation split.

Danish Energy Agency

The DEA gives 2025 planning values at the 2020 price level for a 1.5–2 GW DC cable system (Danish Energy Agency 2025). At 2 GW and restated to 2026 euros, they give:

DEA EPC route 2026-euro reference Interpretation
Subsea DC cable system EUR 8.14 million/km Equipment plus installation/EPC context at 2 GW
Underground DC cable system EUR 10.28 million/km Equipment plus installation/EPC context at 2 GW

These are not installation-only values.

The identical EUR 635,000/km assumptions for an individual infield cable and a complete HVDC system do not demonstrate equal physical installation cost. The export value remains excluded from the model until its scope is reconciled.

The installer benchmark for Vanguard East and West records about EUR 600 million of installation-related cost for 452 km of infield cabling, approximately EUR 1.3 million/km (Installer Benchmark, n.d.). It is an upper contextual comparison because its package scope may include mobilisation, pull-ins, jointing and project overheads.

Installation Sequence

The route-specific model should cover:

  1. surveys, clearance and pre-lay grapnel runs;
  2. cable load-out and transport;
  3. marine laying and simultaneous or post-lay burial;
  4. crossings and external protection;
  5. offshore pull-in and termination support;
  6. landfall, trenching or directional drilling;
  7. terrestrial installation and reinstatement; and
  8. testing and as-laid/as-buried survey.

Bottom-Up Cost Model

\[ C_\mathrm{cable\ installation} = C_\mathrm{mobilisation} +\sum_v r_vt_v +C_\mathrm{landfall} +C_\mathrm{crossings} +C_\mathrm{civil} +C_\mathrm{testing}. \]

The model must distinguish individual cable-kilometres from system route-kilometres and account for separate pole, return and communication cables where applicable.
