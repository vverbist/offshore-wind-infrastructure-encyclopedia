Turbine and Foundation Installation

Role in the Model

This page calculates the offshore installation CAPEX of monopile-supported wind turbines. It treats installation as two independently schedulable campaigns:

  1. monopile and, where applicable, transition-piece or secondary-steel installation; and
  2. turbine transport and erection.

The campaigns share project inputs but have separate vessels, loads, activities, schedules and mobilisation costs. Installation is therefore not represented by a single EUR/MW or EUR/turbine coefficient. Component dimensions first determine vessel feasibility and complete sets per load; countable operations and logistics then determine vessel-days and cost.

ImportantClass changes are explicit

A component that exceeds crane, hook-height, deck, rack or water-depth limits requires another vessel class. A larger component can also reduce the number of complete sets per load and create another port cycle. Neither effect is represented as a smooth duration or cost multiplier.

The installation boundary starts with mobilisation of the contracted installation spread and ends after normal mechanical completion within the stated campaign scope.

Inputs are separated from calculated quantities so that design choices remain visible.

“Effective packed area” includes racks, grillage, access and unusable space. It is not the plan area of the steel alone. When only component geometry is available, the packing factor remains an exposed provisional engineering input.

Each numerical input is identified as a sourced planning input, screening fallback, site-specific input, supplier/contractor input required, or derived result. An unresolved required input blocks the affected result.

Vessel Feasibility

For every component lift \(j\), a candidate vessel must satisfy:

\[ \gamma_\mathrm{dyn}m_j \leq SWL(R_j), \qquad H_\mathrm{hook}\geq H_{\mathrm{required},j}, \]

\[ \sum_j m_j\leq M_\mathrm{deck,use}, \qquad \sum_j a_j\leq A_\mathrm{deck,use}, \qquad p_{\mathrm{local},j}\leq p_\mathrm{deck,max}. \]

Here \(\gamma_\mathrm{dyn}\) is the lift dynamic factor, \(m_j\) is component mass in tonnes, \(SWL(R_j)\) is crane safe working load at the actual lift radius, \(H_\mathrm{hook}\) and \(H_{\mathrm{required},j}\) are in metres, \(M_\mathrm{deck,use}\) is usable payload in tonnes, \(a_j\) and \(A_\mathrm{deck,use}\) are effective component and usable deck areas in square metres, and \(p_\mathrm{local,j}\) and \(p_\mathrm{deck,max}\) are local deck loads in consistent units.

The calculation must also check water depth and leg length, air gap, stability, boom and blade clearance, rack compatibility, motion limits and positioning method. Brochure maximum crane capacity or payload cannot replace the project-specific lift and stowage checks. New-generation vessel specifications demonstrate why the limits are discontinuous: published examples range from more than 2,600 t to 5,000 t crane capacity and differ materially in payload and free deck area (Cadeler, n.d.; DEME Group, n.d.). These are feasibility references, not generic project vessels or day-rate evidence.

Complete Sets per Load

For campaign \(c\in\{F,T\}\), where \(F\) is foundation installation and \(T\) is turbine erection:

\[ n_{\mathrm{set/load},c} = \min\left( \left\lfloor\frac{M_{\mathrm{use},c}}{m_{\mathrm{set},c}}\right\rfloor, \left\lfloor\frac{A_{\mathrm{use},c}}{a_{\mathrm{set},c}}\right\rfloor, n_{\mathrm{rack},c}, n_{\mathrm{stability},c} \right), \]

\[ N_{\mathrm{load},c} = \left\lceil\frac{N}{n_{\mathrm{set/load},c}}\right\rceil, \qquad N_{\mathrm{reload},c}=\max(0,N_{\mathrm{load},c}-1). \]

For foundations, \(m_\mathrm{set,F}=m_\mathrm{MP}+m_\mathrm{TP}\) when both are transported together. For a three-bladed turbine:

\[ m_\mathrm{set,T} = \sum_k m_{\mathrm{tower},k}+m_\mathrm{nacelle}+3m_b. \]

Equivalent component-by-component sums define \(a_\mathrm{set,F}\) and \(a_\mathrm{set,T}\). Quantity-specific rack limits can bind before total mass or area. If \(n_\mathrm{set/load}=0\), the selected vessel and logistics configuration is infeasible.

\(M_\mathrm{use}\) and \(A_\mathrm{use}\) are project-usable deck mass and area; \(m_\mathrm{set}\) and \(a_\mathrm{set}\) are the mass and effective packed area of one complete set; and \(n_\mathrm{rack}\) and \(n_\mathrm{stability}\) are the integer limits imposed by component racks and the approved stowage/stability case. All \(n\) and \(N\) terms are counts.

Foundation-Campaign Duration

Normal onsite activity

For a straightforward driven monopile:

\[ \begin{aligned} t_{F,\mathrm{site}}={}&t_\mathrm{pos}+t_\mathrm{jack}^{\mathrm{up}} +t_\mathrm{survey}+t_\mathrm{release,MP} +\frac{L_\mathrm{MP}}{v_\mathrm{upend}} +\frac{d_w}{v_\mathrm{lower}}\\ &+t_\mathrm{tool}+\frac{L_e}{q_\mathrm{drive,eff}} +t_\mathrm{TP}+t_\mathrm{jack}^{\mathrm{down}}. \end{aligned} \]

All terms are converted to days before addition. \(q_\mathrm{drive,eff}\) is the effective penetration rate for the stated pile, soil, hammer and method. Its scope must say whether normal pauses and soft-start are included. Relief drilling, refusal remediation and project-specific noise restrictions are excluded unless explicitly represented.

Symbol Definition Unit
\(t_\mathrm{pos}\) Vessel positioning at one location h/location
\(t_\mathrm{jack}^{\mathrm{up}}\), \(t_\mathrm{jack}^{\mathrm{down}}\) Raising and lowering the jack-up hull and legs as applicable h/location
\(t_\mathrm{survey}\) Routine pre-installation survey h/location
\(t_\mathrm{release,MP}\) Release of one monopile from its sea fastening h/monopile
\(v_\mathrm{upend}\), \(v_\mathrm{lower}\) Effective monopile upending and lowering rates m/h
\(t_\mathrm{tool}\) Piling-tool handling and changeover h/location
\(q_\mathrm{drive,eff}\) Effective normal penetration rate for the defined pile-soil-hammer system m/h
\(t_\mathrm{TP}\) Critical-path transition-piece installation time h/transition piece

For a separate transition piece:

\[ t_\mathrm{TP} = t_\mathrm{release,TP}+t_\mathrm{reequip} +\frac{H_\mathrm{lower,TP}}{v_\mathrm{crane}} + \begin{cases} t_\mathrm{bolt}, & \text{bolted interface},\\ t_\mathrm{grout,pump}+t_\mathrm{grout,cure,critical}, & \text{grouted interface}. \end{cases} \]

Only critical-path grout curing occupies the primary vessel. An extended monopile omits \(t_\mathrm{TP}\), but any later secondary-steel package must remain in the installation inventory.

\(t_\mathrm{release,TP}\) is transition-piece release time; \(t_\mathrm{reequip}\) is crane or tool reconfiguration time; \(H_\mathrm{lower,TP}\) and \(v_\mathrm{crane}\) are lowering path in metres and applicable crane rate in metres per hour; and \(t_\mathrm{bolt}\), \(t_\mathrm{grout,pump}\) and \(t_\mathrm{grout,cure,critical}\) are the relevant connection times in hours.

NREL’s ORBIT methodology provides a transparent public task decomposition. It uses 2 h for positioning, 1 h for routine ROV survey, 3 h for monopile release, 1 h for crane re-equipping, 4 h for bolting, and 2 h pumping plus 24 h curing for a grouted interface (Nunemaker et al. 2020). Its port assumptions are 12 h fastening per monopile and 8 h per transition piece. These are planning-model assumptions, not universal observed durations. ORBIT represents simple driving and does not establish a transferable site penetration rate.

Exceptional foundation routes

A geotechnical requirement for relief drilling or drive-drill-drive creates a separate activity or installation class. It is not hidden by increasing normal site time. The exceptional-work structure is:

\[ C_\mathrm{exceptional,F} = N_\mathrm{drill}r_\mathrm{drill}t_\mathrm{drill} +N_\mathrm{noise}C_\mathrm{noise/unit} +A_\mathrm{scour}c_\mathrm{scour} +N_\mathrm{boulder}C_\mathrm{boulder} +C_\mathrm{UXO}+C_\mathrm{remediation}. \]

The rates and quantities are site-specific. No transferable public unit costs were identified for these activities with a sufficiently consistent technical and commercial boundary.

\(N_\mathrm{drill}\), \(N_\mathrm{noise}\) and \(N_\mathrm{boulder}\) are affected-location counts; \(r_\mathrm{drill}\) and \(t_\mathrm{drill}\) are drilling-spread rate and duration; \(C_\mathrm{noise/unit}\) and \(C_\mathrm{boulder}\) are defined unit costs; \(A_\mathrm{scour}\) and \(c_\mathrm{scour}\) are scour-treatment area and unit cost; and \(C_\mathrm{UXO}\) and \(C_\mathrm{remediation}\) are separately estimated campaign costs. Each cost is in constant 2026 EUR after normalization.

Turbine-Erection Duration

Let \(J\) contain every tower section, the nacelle or rotor-nacelle assembly, and each blade. The calm-condition component-count model is:

\[ t_{T,\mathrm{site,calm}} = t_\mathrm{pos}+t_\mathrm{jack}^{\mathrm{up}}+t_\mathrm{jack}^{\mathrm{down}} +\sum_{j\in J}N_j \left( t_\mathrm{release,j} +\frac{H_\mathrm{path,j}}{v_\mathrm{crane,j}} +t_\mathrm{attach,j} \right) +t_\mathrm{reequip}. \]

Hub height drives lift path and hook-height feasibility. Rotor diameter drives blade length, rack footprint, clearance and wind sensitivity. Component mass drives capacity at radius, rigging and deck loading. Without a documented crane load-speed curve, the model does not invent a continuous mass-to-lift-time coefficient.

\(N_j\) is the count of component type \(j\) per turbine; \(t_\mathrm{release,j}\) and \(t_\mathrm{attach,j}\) are release and attachment times in hours per component; \(H_\mathrm{path,j}\) is lift path in metres; and \(v_\mathrm{crane,j}\) is the applicable crane rate in metres per hour. \(t_\mathrm{reequip}\) is any non-component-specific reconfiguration time not already included in those tasks.

ORBIT’s public planning assumptions are (Nunemaker et al. 2020):

Component or task Port fastening Offshore release Offshore attachment
Tower set 4 h/set 3 h/set 6 h/set
Nacelle 4 h/component 3 h/component 6 h/component
Blade 1.5 h/blade 1 h/blade 3.5 h/blade
Vessel positioning — 2 h/location —
Crane re-equipping — 1 h/location —

A standard three-bladed, single-tower-set configuration therefore has 12.5 h of component fastening per turbine set before any tower-section or port-queue adjustment. Lift time remains height divided by the applicable crane rate.

Weather is represented either by explicit accessibility:

\[ t_{T,\mathrm{site,eff}} = \frac{t_{T,\mathrm{site,calm}}}{A_\mathrm{lift}}, \qquad 0<A_\mathrm{lift}\leq 1, \]

or by a weather-inclusive effective task duration. The two treatments cannot be combined. Accessibility should match the installation month, vessel limits and wind-critical lift at hub height; a daily mean wind speed is not an adequate proxy for blade-lift access.

Transport and Supply Logistics

Self-shuttling installation vessel

For campaign \(c\):

\[ T_{c,\mathrm{primary}} = t_{\mathrm{setup},c}+Nt_{c,\mathrm{site}} +N_{\mathrm{load},c}t_{\mathrm{load},c} +N_{\mathrm{load},c}\frac{2D_p}{v_{\mathrm{transit},c}} +(N-1)\frac{D_i}{v_{\mathrm{field},c}}. \]

\(t_\mathrm{load,c}\) contains the applicable component fastening and defined port operations. Transit and activity units are converted consistently to days.

\(t_\mathrm{setup,c}\) is campaign setup time in days, \(v_\mathrm{transit,c}\) and \(v_\mathrm{field,c}\) are port-transit and inter-site speeds in kilometres per day, and \(D_p\) and \(D_i\) are one-way port distance and mean inter-site distance in kilometres.

Feeder-supplied installation vessel

When the primary installation vessel remains offshore, installation and supply capacities are calculated separately:

\[ Q_\mathrm{install} = \frac{1}{t_{c,\mathrm{site}}+D_i/v_\mathrm{field}}, \]

\[ Q_\mathrm{supply} = \frac{n_\mathrm{feeder}n_\mathrm{set/load},c} {t_\mathrm{load,feeder}}+2D_p/v_\mathrm{feeder}+t_\mathrm{transfer}}, \]

\[ T_{c,\mathrm{primary}} = t_{\mathrm{setup},c} +\frac{N}{\min(Q_\mathrm{install},Q_\mathrm{supply})}. \]

Feeder vessel-days are calculated from their own cycles. Feeder time is not serially added to primary-vessel time when the vessels operate concurrently, but the occupied days of both vessel classes are costed.

\(Q_\mathrm{install}\) and \(Q_\mathrm{supply}\) are installation and supply capacities in sets per day; \(n_\mathrm{feeder}\) is feeder count; \(v_\mathrm{feeder}\) is feeder transit speed; and \(t_\mathrm{load,feeder}\) and \(t_\mathrm{transfer}\) are complete feeder port-turnaround and offshore-transfer times. Their boundary must state whether waiting and ordinary weather are included.

Campaign Cost

With individually priced vessels, campaign cost is:

\[ C_c = C_\mathrm{mob},c +r_\mathrm{primary},cT_\mathrm{primary},c +\sum_v r_{v,c}T_{v,c} +C_\mathrm{consumables},c. \]

Total turbine and foundation installation CAPEX is:

\[ C_\mathrm{WT,inst} = \sum_{c\in\{F,T\}}C_c +C_\mathrm{exceptional,F} +C_\mathrm{other}. \]

Two rate conventions are valid:

  • Complete spread: the rate includes the primary vessel and every named ordinary support item.
  • Vessel stack: primary vessel, feeder, survey/ROV, noise-mitigation vessel and other support are costed separately.

The model records which convention is used and what it includes. It must not add vessel-stack items already contained in a complete spread.

\(C_\mathrm{mob,c}\) is campaign mobilisation and demobilisation cost, \(r_\mathrm{primary,c}\) and \(r_{v,c}\) are constant-2026-EUR day rates, \(T_\mathrm{primary,c}\) and \(T_{v,c}\) are chargeable vessel-days, and \(C_\mathrm{consumables,c}\) contains only named installation consumables. \(C_\mathrm{other}\) is reserved for separately enumerated installation work that belongs within this page’s boundary but fits neither a normal campaign nor the defined foundation exceptions; an unexplained lump sum is not permitted.

The three foundation durations are coherent screening fallbacks and validation bands, not multipliers to apply after the activity model. The source assumes 24-hour operations without weather or sea delay and approximately 2–4 days per monopile foundation, including a 2–4 h driving interval (South Fork Wind, LLC 2021). The activity calculation takes precedence when its inputs are available.

For turbine erection, the scenarios change vessel class, sets per load, logistics and accessibility rather than inventing favourable, typical and difficult point durations.

Constant-2026-EUR Normalization

Source cost, escalation and currency conversion remain separate:

\[ C_{2026,\mathrm{EUR}} = C_{y,\mathrm{cur}} \frac{I_{2026}}{I_y} FX_{\mathrm{cur}\rightarrow\mathrm{EUR},2026}. \]

For the ORBIT 2024-USD vessel proxies, the fallback uses 2024 annual-average CPI-U of 313.689, June 2026 CPI-U of 333.952, and the 30 July 2026 ECB reference rate of EUR 1 = USD 1.1476 (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics 2026b, 2026a; European Central Bank 2026):

\[ F_{2024\,\mathrm{USD}\rightarrow2026\,\mathrm{EUR}} = \frac{333.952}{313.689}\frac{1}{1.1476} =0.9276715. \]

Applied to USD 400,000/day and USD 93,692/day, this gives EUR 371,069/day and EUR 86,915/day. CPI is transparent but does not reproduce a tight offshore-vessel market. A documented offshore-construction or vessel index is preferable when its scope and licensing permit. Geographic-market adjustments remain separate.

Reported Intermediate Results

The model must return enough information to reproduce and interrogate the total.

Result group Required outputs
Feasibility Pass/fail and margin for crane at radius, hook height, deck mass, deck area, local load, racks, water depth and operating limits
Physical inventory Turbine locations, foundation sets, turbine sets and component lifts
Logistics Sets per load, loads, reloads, port cycles, feeder cycles and inter-site moves by campaign
Duration Port, transit, positioning/jacking, handling, penetration, lift/attachment, weather treatment and total vessel-days
Cost Mobilisation, primary vessel, feeder/support, consumables, exceptional work and total by campaign
Evidence Value, unit, source or assumption status, scope, price year and normalization record for each input

A EUR/MW or EUR/turbine result may be reported only as a derived comparison after these quantities have been calculated.

Validation Cases and Assertions

Public projects test physical quantities and elapsed schedules; they do not provide clean, chargeable vessel-day costs.

Case What it tests Scope limitation
Sofia foundations: 100 extended monopiles, three per vessel cycle, approximately 195 km offshore (RWE Offshore Wind GmbH 2024, 2025) Load count, long-distance logistics and elapsed campaign productivity Calendar period includes weather, support and interruptions
Sofia turbines: 100 14 MW turbines with 222 m rotors, 108 m blades and six sets per load (Sofia Offshore Wind Farm 2026) Turbine sets per load and remote-project calendar productivity Calendar duration is not net WTIV occupation
Baltic Power: 78 monopiles of approximately 1,300–1,700 t and more than 9 m diameter (ORLEN Group 2025, 2026) Crane, payload and support-fleet class No separated installation cost or net vessel days
NREL 15 MW and 22 MW reference turbines (Zahle et al. 2024) Hook-height, deck-load and sets-per-load sensitivity as turbines scale Reference designs, not observed projects
ORBIT worked example (National Laboratory of the Rockies, n.d.) Equation implementation and cost-boundary reconciliation Synthetic design and model defaults

The implementation must assert that:

  • every selected vessel passes all required feasibility gates;
  • \(n_\mathrm{set/load}\geq1\) and each load count is an integer;
  • the initial load is not counted as a reload;
  • weather is represented by accessibility or weather-inclusive effective time, never both;
  • concurrent feeder time is not added serially to primary-vessel duration;
  • each support vessel is inside the spread rate or costed separately;
  • relief drilling and other exceptional work are outside normal simple-driving time; and
  • every cost has a currency, price year, normalization record and stated inclusions.

Limitations and Required Evidence

Unresolved evidence Consequence
No current auditable North Sea complete-spread quotation Public vessel rates remain screening proxies
No transferable relationship between pile dimensions and penetration rate independent of soil and hammer Foundation duration requires project geotechnical input
No documented universal relationship between component mass and crane speed Lift time requires the actual crane curve or contractor duration
No transferable relationship between rotor size and weather downtime Turbine access requires site-, month- and operation-specific analysis
No public project split into net chargeable vessel-days with consistent inclusions Public projects validate intermediate quantities only
No transferable public rates for drilling, noise mitigation, scour, boulder or UXO work Exceptional work remains site-specific

Before reporting a commercial installation CAPEX, obtain from the contractor or vessel owner: the named vessel and complete rate boundary; capacity at the required radius and hook height; project-usable deck and rack limits; port and offshore task times; ordinary-weather treatment; pile-soil-hammer penetration performance; feeder requirement; and the charging rules for standby, cancellation, repairs and overruns.
