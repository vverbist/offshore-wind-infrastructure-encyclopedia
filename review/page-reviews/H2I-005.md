## Executive assessment

This is a useful synthesis page. It clearly explains why pressure, diameter,
pipeline count and installation subclass must be evaluated together and why a
smooth one-variable optimisation would miss material cost steps. Its emphasis
on physical pipes, interfaces and passes is consistent with the project's
minimum-sufficient-fidelity principles.

It is not an executable optimisation specification. Bounds, candidate product
classes, constraints, objective evaluation and tie-breaking remain absent on
the methodology pages. Two technical statements also need qualification: gas
capacity depends on the difference of squared absolute pressures and gas
properties, not only a linear pressure drop under a square root; and the 8 inch
boundary is a product/installation qualification transition rather than a
universal internal-diameter limit.

## 1. Model role and boundary

- **Purpose:** reconcile compressor, pipeline and installation costs and make
  the discrete pressure/diameter/count search logic visible.
- **Inputs:** candidate compressor outlet pressure, qualified pipeline classes,
  peak flow, route, material cost and installation campaign outputs.
- **Output:** the feasible candidate set and its total lifecycle cost/LCOE.
- **Ownership:** equations remain on H2I-003/H2I-004 and the search convention
  remains on METH-005.

## 2. Calculation reconstruction

For every qualified diameter/pressure-class candidate, the intended model
should:

1. calculate compressor energy and architecture-specific train CAPEX;
2. calculate pipeline capacity at the required receipt condition;
3. derive the ceiling-based physical pipeline count;
4. build the material and connection inventory;
5. determine the qualified installation subclass and pass count;
6. calculate lifecycle cost and delivered hydrogen; and
7. reject infeasible candidates before comparing the objective.

The page conveys this sequence qualitatively but supplies none of the search
sets or executable constraints.

## 3. Technical correctness and model fidelity

The `D^2.5` leading dependence and pipeline-count discontinuity are appropriate
screening insights. The pressure explanation is incomplete: the cited General
Flow Equation uses `sqrt(p_in^2-p_out^2)` and includes compressibility and
temperature. The page's “pressure enters only through pressure drop” wording
also conflicts with H2I-004, which correctly notes pressure-dependent gas
properties.

The model should retain this page as interpretation rather than duplicate the
canonical equations. Once H2I-004 is corrected, this page can link to the
qualified expression and focus on the discrete economic consequences.

## 4. Cost and evidence audit

No independent cost input is introduced. The conclusion depends completely on
the unresolved compressor correlation, confidential pipeline cost regression
and unparameterized TCP installation campaign. A calculated optimum must not
be reported until all three cost centres use compatible price and physical
boundaries.

## 5. Independent-implementer test

- **Can the page be implemented?** No; it describes a search but not its
  candidate sets, constraints or result-selection rule.
- **Missing numerical evidence:** qualified product and installation classes
  and all unresolved component costs.
- **Missing decisions:** variable taxonomy, public/confidential cost basis,
  pressure-class and compressor-train conventions.
- **Missing rules:** infeasibility, extrapolation, tie-breaking and
  uncertainty across pipeline-count thresholds.

## 6. Explanation and site placement

The page is concise and located correctly after the compressor and pipeline
pages. It adds value as a synthesis and should not be merged. Its role should
be labelled “trade-off and search logic” rather than read as another
calculation owner. The pipeline-installation statement here is the correct
one-pipe-per-pass convention and exposes the inconsistency on H2I-001.
