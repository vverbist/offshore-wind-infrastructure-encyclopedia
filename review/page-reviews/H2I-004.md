## Executive assessment

The page has the right high-level decomposition. It separates material supply
from installation, exposes diameter/pressure/length dependencies, preserves
parallel-pipeline count as a ceiling function and correctly avoids treating
pipeline material cost alone as the pressure optimum.

The adopted physical and cost model is not yet defensible. The hydraulic code
mixes an incompressible pressure-drop/average-density relation with the cited
gas-pipeline method, which uses the difference in squared absolute pressures.
It also omits velocity and product-qualification gates. Diameter is called
internal diameter while the 8 inch technology limit is a nominal/product-size
claim; a public TCP product example identifies 8 inch NPS with up to 7.5 inch
internal diameter. That distinction changes capacity materially.

The confidential cost model is described as interpolation but is a global
bilinear least-squares regression. A controlled check against its quote points
gives about 19% mean absolute percentage error and a maximum error of about
51%. It also prices a continuous operating pressure rather than selecting a
qualified discrete pressure class. The public page and tracked repository do
not contain the numerical surface, price basis or sufficient validation for an
independent result.

## 1. Model role and boundary

- **Purpose:** qualify and size infield/export TCPs, calculate transport
  capacity and physical count, and calculate material CAPEX.
- **Architectures:** export pipeline for both hydrogen architectures; infield
  network for decentralised hydrogen.
- **Included:** steady-state capacity, material cost, annual material-CAPEX
  OPEX and availability convention.
- **Excluded:** installation, manifold, landfall/terminal equipment and
  onshore recompression.
- **Boundary concern:** the stated project endpoint requires a backbone
  connection package that is not assigned or costed (ISS-0028).

## 3. Calculation reconstruction

The page currently:

1. converts a selected inch value directly to internal diameter;
2. sets a linear pressure difference and a special average pressure;
3. obtains density and viscosity at that pressure from CoolProp;
4. derives `Re sqrt(f_F)` and evaluates a Fanning Colebrook expression;
5. calculates velocity and mass flow using an average density;
6. converts flow to HHV-equivalent MW;
7. ceilings peak flow by capacity to obtain pipeline count; and
8. evaluates a confidential diameter/pressure cost surface and multiplies by
   physical route length and count.

The cited [Transition Accelerator General Flow Equation](https://transitionaccelerator.ca/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/The-Techno-Economics-of-Hydrogen-Pipelines-v2.pdf) instead depends on
`sqrt(p_in^2 - p_out^2)` using absolute pressures and a Darcy friction factor.
Its average-pressure equation is supplied for gas properties; it does not by
itself justify replacing the gas-flow relation with a linear pressure drop and
one average density. The current alternative needs an independent derivation
and benchmark before use.

### Boundary and limiting-case checks

- Require absolute pressures and `p_in > p_out`; convert gauge interface values
  explicitly.
- Reject diameter/pressure pairs without a named qualified product class.
- Distinguish nominal outside/product size from hydraulic internal diameter.
- Check Reynolds regime, maximum/erosional velocity and any minimum delivery
  margin.
- Preserve discrete diameter and pressure classes, pipeline count, fittings,
  field connections, reel loads and installation subclasses.
- Reject extrapolation outside the supplier evidence domain.

## 5. Technical correctness

Verified elements:

- the area and HHV conversion equations are dimensionally correct;
- the Fanning form of Colebrook uses the expected factor-of-four relation to
  the Darcy convention; and
- the `D^2.5` leading dependence and discrete pipeline-count principle are
  appropriate for screening.

Material concerns:

- the cited source explicitly uses squared absolute pressures, whereas the
  implementation uses a different average-density surrogate without
  validation (ISS-0084);
- [Hynetwork specifies 66.2 bar(g)](https://www.hynetwork.nl/en/business/become-a-customer/specifications) as the Dutch network design/maximum operating pressure, while
  the current temporary operating range is 30–50 bar(g). Using 66 bar without
  gauge/absolute conversion or an injection margin is not a receipt
  specification (ISS-0085);
- the 6 inch, 150-to-66 bar, 80 km code case is about 437 MW HHV, not 500 MW;
  it cannot establish infield section capacity because length and downstream
  pressure differ by section; and
- no velocity or TCP pressure/temperature/service qualification gate is
  implemented.

## 6. Model fidelity

A steady isothermal screening calculation with real-gas properties is
appropriate; transient linepack and detailed thermal/elevation modelling can
remain outside the base case. Minimum sufficient fidelity still requires a
named product class, its true internal diameter, pressure/temperature envelope,
discrete pressure class, section length and a velocity/qualification screen.

Continuous cost interpolation over operating pressure is not appropriate when
the supplied pipe is purchased in discrete rated-pressure constructions. The
model should select the least qualified class at or above design pressure and
preserve each class and installation transition.

## 8. Independent-implementer test

- **Can the page be implemented?** The visible capacity equations can be
  coded, but their validity is unresolved; the cost result cannot be
  independently reproduced.
- **Missing evidence:** qualified H2 TCP product, hydraulic diameter,
  pressure/temperature/life envelope, fittings, material price year/scope,
  OPEX and failure/repair data.
- **Missing decisions:** pressure classes, receipt condition, acceptable use of
  confidential inputs and public validation/fallback.
- **Missing rules:** gauge-to-absolute conversion, design pressure margin,
  product-class selection, velocity limits, cost-fit validation and
  extrapolation behavior.
- **Missing physical data:** linear mass, outside diameter, bend radius,
  continuous reel length, connector count, termination dimensions and
  installation class.

## 9. Explanation and site placement

The page is well structured and the material-versus-installation boundary is
clear. It is the correct canonical owner for pipeline hydraulics and supply.
The principal clarity defect is that declared limitations appear after figures
that look like adopted results. A future correction should lead with a compact
qualification/status box and distinguish operating pressure, design pressure,
rated pressure class, gauge pressure and absolute pressure.
