## Executive assessment

The BOP scope boundary is strong and the source cost breakdown is clear. The
base case nevertheless uses an explicitly labelled offshore-water placeholder
and applies a generic 0.6 exponent from a 1 MW land-based aggregate to
multi-MW/GW offshore systems. This scaling can dominate the centralised versus
decentralised result without evidence for train sizes, redundancy or
architecture. The documented constant 1.0 kWh/kg energy model also differs
materially from the load-dependent legacy implementation.

## 1. Model role and boundary

- **Purpose:** cost and power the non-electrical electrolyser auxiliaries,
  including offshore water treatment.
- **Included:** water, cooling, gas-liquid separation, drying, piping,
  instrumentation and housing.
- **Excluded:** stack, power electronics, compression, platform and installation.
- **Ownership:** boundary is explicit; mass output is missing.

## 3. Calculation reconstruction

Each BOP unit is costed from a 1 MW reference with exponent 0.6 and summed.
Auxiliary energy is $E_\mathrm{BOP}=e_\mathrm{BOP}m_\mathrm{H2}$. The page does
not specify a solution sequence for the implicit relationship between hydrogen
output and parasitic power, nor practical maximum train size/count.

### Boundary and limiting-case checks

- Zero capacity should give zero cost; exponent model is undefined for negative
  capacity.
- Train count and redundancy must create discrete steps.
- Scaling should not extrapolate beyond an evidenced range without branching.
- Auxiliary power cannot exceed available electrolyser input.

## 5. Technical correctness

The cost arithmetic (222 × 1.5 = 333; 42 × 1.5 = 63) and desalination example
(10 L/kg × 5 kWh/m3 = 0.05 kWh/kg) are correct. The generic six-tenths rule is
not evidence that the combined offshore BOP can scale from one 1 MW reference
to a single GW-scale train. Manufacturing volume and equipment/plant scale are
distinct effects.

## 6. Model fidelity

Process-level modelling of every auxiliary is unnecessary now. Minimum
sufficient fidelity is a small number of explicit BOP packages/trains with
capacity limits, redundancy, item-level or grouped exponents, and a documented
part-load auxiliary model. The current smooth aggregate creates an unsupported
architecture advantage.

## 7. Cost and evidence audit

NREL supports the land-based manufactured-cost components. The 50% markup is a
project assumption. Offshore water treatment is explicitly a placeholder and
cannot be a base input under the project's evidence rules. The 0.6 exponent is
supported only as a generic preliminary heuristic, not for the complete scope
or extrapolation range. Legacy code instead uses a 15 MW reference and 364.5
per kW, so documentation and execution also disagree.

## 8. Independent-implementer test

The written formula can be implemented, but it does not define a defensible
architecture model. Train sizing, water cost, power method, price
normalisation, mass and code alignment are missing.

## 9. Explanation and site placement

Scope tables are excellent. Equations use literal square brackets and symbols
in parentheses rather than Quarto math delimiters, degrading rendered clarity.
