## Executive assessment

The page adopts the right model structure: compressor work responds to inlet
pressure, outlet pressure and hydrogen flow; stage count is discrete; energy
is a parasitic load; and central and turbine-level compressor costs are kept
separate. The two-stage 30-to-150 bar example and dimensional conversions are
correct.

The cost and equipment model is not reproducible. `C_comp,ref` and
`P_comp,ref` are never numerically defined, the only 56 EUR/kW anchor is
reported per electrolyser capacity rather than compressor motor power, and no
currency/price-year or offshore boundary is stated. The cited source limits a
large pipeline compressor to 16,000 kW and assumes multiple units above that
rating, whereas the page applies one smooth scale-economy curve to the full
farm compressor. This can materially predetermine the centralisation result.

## 1. Model role and boundary

- **Purpose:** calculate compression electricity, motor rating, CAPEX, OPEX
  and availability as pipeline injection pressure changes.
- **Architectures:** one farm-level subsystem for central hydrogen and one
  subsystem per turbine for decentralised hydrogen.
- **Included:** multistage compression and motor loss.
- **Excluded:** drying, piping pressure drop, onshore recompression and
  offshore structural/integration cost.
- **Boundary risk:** the cited compressor correlation is total installed cost
  with an onshore installation factor, while platform and offshore
  installation are separately owned.

## 3. Calculation reconstruction

For valid `p_out > p_in > 0`, the page:

1. calculates stage count with a ceiling on total pressure ratio;
2. assigns equal pressure ratio to each stage;
3. calculates ideal-gas isentropic work corrected by `Z` and compressor
   efficiency;
4. divides by motor efficiency and converts J/kg to kWh/kg;
5. multiplies by mass flow to obtain motor power; and
6. applies a sub-linear power-law cost separately to the farm train or every
   turbine unit.

The equation is the multistage isentropic relation with perfect intercooling
back to the original suction temperature. Those assumptions must be stated.
Calling the 0.80 term only “compressor efficiency” obscures that it is
isentropic efficiency.

### Boundary and limiting-case checks

- `p_out <= p_in` should return zero compression only when bypass is explicitly
  allowed; it must not enter the logarithmic stage equation.
- Stage count must be at least one for a positive compression duty and preserve
  each ratio threshold.
- Per-stage discharge temperature and product limits should be screening
  assertions even when intercooling is idealised.
- Motor rating and train count must use peak simultaneous flow, while annual
  electricity uses time-step flow.
- Central train count must step when duty exceeds the adopted per-train limit;
  the cost curve must not be extrapolated as one compressor.

## 5. Technical correctness

The stage-count and equal-ratio equations are correct. With the figure-builder
implementation, 30-to-150 bar compression is approximately 0.86 kWh/kg and
uses two stages. The 1-to-150 bar curve uses five stages and is about 3.2 times
the 30 bar case, consistent with the page's qualitative statement.

The property treatment is inconsistent. The page/figure builder evaluates one
`Z` at arithmetic mean pressure, while legacy code averages inlet and outlet
`Z`; the cited worked method uses an average pressure and temperature and
states perfect intercooling. This difference is small in the present range but
is an undocumented model change. A single controlled property method and
CoolProp version is required.

## 6. Model fidelity

An explicit multistage screening equation is sufficient; compressor maps and
detailed thermal design are not needed. The missing minimum fidelity is
modularity. The cited source differentiates high-flow centrifugal pipeline
compressors from small high-ratio units, uses a maximum 16 MW rating for the
large unit and assumes multiple compressors above the limit. Applying one
unbounded exponent to farm scale and the same correlation to turbine-scale
units erases technology and train-count steps and mechanically rewards
centralisation.

## 8. Independent-implementer test

- **Can the page be implemented?** Energy approximately; cost, architecture
  scaling and physical integration cannot.
- **Missing numerical evidence:** cost coefficient/price basis, train ratings,
  offshore packaging, OPEX and availability.
- **Missing decisions:** compressor technology and package for each
  architecture, redundancy and spare philosophy.
- **Missing rules:** peak-duty sizing, number of trains, turndown, standby
  capacity, code/property method and failure states.
- **Missing outputs:** equipment mass, footprint, dimensions and lifts.

## 9. Explanation and site placement

The calculation order and cross-links are good. The cost section reads as a
complete model despite omitting the only constants needed to calculate cost.
The future correction should put a compact adopted-input ledger before the
equation and replace the generic “compressor efficiency” label with
“isentropic efficiency”. No page move is needed.
