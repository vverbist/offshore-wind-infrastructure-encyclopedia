## Executive assessment

The page explains why part-load efficiency, modular switching, overplanting
and degradation matter. It does not provide the numerical polarisation/
efficiency data required to reproduce hydrogen yield. The page uses 1 MW
modules while the legacy implementation uses 2.5 MW modules, its degradation
example is arithmetically wrong, replacement is described but not implemented,
and three incompatible stack-cost bases appear. Stack mass is also missing.

## 1. Model role and boundary

- **Purpose:** convert stack DC power into hydrogen and track part-load,
  modularity, degradation, availability, CAPEX and mass.
- **Architectures:** both hydrogen architectures.
- **Included:** stack electrochemistry and replacement.
- **Excluded:** power electronics, non-electrical BOP and compression.

## 3. Calculation reconstruction

The intended order is: choose installed stack/module count; select active
modules for each power point; obtain efficiency from the active-module load;
convert DC energy to hydrogen on an HHV basis; accumulate full-load hours;
degrade efficiency; trigger and cost replacement; apply non-overlapping
availability. The page omits the numerical curve and exact dispatch/replacement
algorithm.

### Boundary and limiting-case checks

- Below minimum module power, output should be zero or a defined standby state.
- Module count and replacement are discrete.
- Switching tie-breaks, hysteresis and cycling limits need rules.
- Efficiency and current must remain inside the source curve; no extrapolation.
- Replacement cash flow must reset the appropriate degradation state.

## 5. Technical correctness

The load-sharing concept is sound. The EOL arithmetic is not:
$10\%/(0.18\%/1{,}000\,\mathrm{h})=55{,}556$ FLH, not 58,500 FLH; at 40%
capacity factor this is about 15.9 years, not the implication of the printed
number. “At least one replacement” is not universally true because realised
FLH depends on dispatch and overplanting.

## 6. Model fidelity

Modular part-load dispatch is material and appropriate. Switching penalties
can remain excluded initially if declared and sensitivity-tested. Degradation
and replacement must be stateful or consistently approximated; the current
code applies average degradation and no replacement, which is not the page's
model.

## 7. Cost and evidence audit

The NREL manufactured-cost study is appropriate, but the page alternates
between 164 USD2020/kW, a derived 112 USD2020/kW at altered current density,
and “100 EUR/kW (CHECK)” after markup/conversion. The last result cannot follow
from 112 × 1.5 before inflation under ordinary exchange rates. Legacy cost is
112.5 per kW with no documented price/currency basis. No adopted EUR2025 stack
input exists.

## 8. Independent-implementer test

No: the curve, module basis, dispatch, degradation/replacement state, adopted
cost and mass are missing or contradictory. These are model inputs, not optional
background.

## 9. Explanation and site placement

The conceptual sequence is readable, but the page needs a single adopted
parameter table and a machine-readable curve. Remove unfinished “CHECK” text
from any base-case section after the decision is made.
