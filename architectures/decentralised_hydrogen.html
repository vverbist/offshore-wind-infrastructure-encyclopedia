Decentralised Hydrogen

Architecture Definition

The decentralised-hydrogen architecture converts electrical energy to hydrogen at each wind turbine. Hydrogen rather than electricity is collected within the wind farm, exported through a subsea pipeline, and handed over at the connection to the onshore hydrogen backbone.

The physical boundary includes the wind turbines and support structures, turbine-integrated hydrogen-production equipment, turbine-level compression, the infield hydrogen network, export pipeline, and onshore backbone-connection interface. It contains no central offshore conversion platform.

Turbine-Integrated Conversion Boundary

The turbine-side active front end and DC link remain part of the conversion chain. The PEM stack connects to that DC link without a separate electrolyser-side DC/DC converter.

The turbine-level electrolyser facility includes:

  • PEM stack modules
  • non-electrical electrolyser balance of plant
  • local seawater intake, desalination, and electrolyser-grade water treatment

The turbine-level compressor is physically associated with the same turbine unit but remains a separate downstream functional and accounting subsystem. It is not included in the electrolyser or balance-of-plant boundary.

Function Treatment Canonical owner
Wind-energy capture and generation Included Wind Turbine
Turbine support structure Included Foundation
Turbine rectification and DC-link interface Included Generator and Power Electronics

PEM stacks Included at each turbine PEM Electrolyser Stack

Installation of the turbines, foundations, turbine-integrated hydrogen equipment, and pipelines remains inside the economic boundary. Installation assumptions are maintained in the Offshore Installation section.

Removed or Excluded Functions

The following functions are not part of the decentralised architecture:

  • the turbine grid-side inverter and step-up transformer in the hydrogen path
  • a separate electrolyser-side DC/DC converter
  • the AC infield collection network
  • a central offshore substation or electrolysis platform
  • central electrolyser transformer and rectifier equipment
  • offshore and onshore HVDC conversion
  • HVDC cable export
  • the onshore hydrogen backbone beyond the project connection
  • downstream hydrogen storage, distribution, reconversion, and end use

Architecture Interfaces

Interface Boundary definition
Generator to electrolysis The turbine-side active front end supplies the DC link; the PEM stack is connected without a separate DC/DC stage.
Electrolyser facility to compression Produced and conditioned hydrogen leaves the turbine-level electrolyser boundary and enters the separately accounted compressor.
Turbine unit to infield network Compressed hydrogen enters the infield pipeline network.
Infield network to export system Collected hydrogen enters the export pipeline system.
Export to backbone The project ends at the onshore hydrogen-backbone connection.

Economic Boundary

The architecture cost includes the common offshore wind project and all turbine-level hydrogen-production, compression, collection, export, and installation activities required to reach the backbone connection. Removing the AC collection and central platform functions changes which components are booked; it does not remove the common turbine, foundation, development, or financing boundary.

The hydrogen backbone and any downstream reinforcement or conditioning are outside the base architecture boundary unless a scenario explicitly assigns them to the offshore project.
