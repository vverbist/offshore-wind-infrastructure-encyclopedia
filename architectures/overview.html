Architectures

This section defines the scope and boundary of the three offshore wind architectures compared by the model:

  1. electricity export using AC infield collection and HVDC export
  2. centralised offshore hydrogen production with one integrated offshore platform
  3. decentralised turbine-integrated hydrogen production

The architecture pages identify which physical functions are present, where energy conversion occurs, and how the offshore system connects to the onshore delivery point. Component pages remain the owners of sizing methods, assumptions, costs, losses, availability, and technical limitations.

Comparison Boundary

The common offshore-project boundary starts with the wind turbine and its support structure. It ends at:

  • the onshore electricity-grid connection for electricity export; or
  • the connection to the onshore hydrogen backbone for hydrogen export.

Infrastructure beyond those connection points is outside the model boundary. This excludes the wider electricity network, the hydrogen backbone itself, downstream storage, distribution, reconversion, and end use.

Architecture Distinctions

Question Electricity export Centralised hydrogen Decentralised hydrogen
Carrier in the infield network Electricity Electricity Hydrogen
Offshore conversion location Central HVDC platform One central electrolysis platform At each turbine
Carrier exported to shore Electricity Hydrogen Hydrogen
Central offshore platform AC aggregation and HVDC conversion AC aggregation and electrolysis None
Onshore delivery point Electricity-grid connection Hydrogen-backbone connection Hydrogen-backbone connection

The centralised-hydrogen architecture has one physical offshore platform. AC aggregation, electrolyser power electronics, PEM stacks, water treatment, and the non-electrical electrolyser balance of plant are integrated on that platform. Compression is a separate functional and accounting subsystem, even when it is physically hosted on the same platform.

In the decentralised-hydrogen architecture, the PEM stack connects to the turbine DC link without a separate electrolyser-side DC/DC converter. Water treatment belongs to the turbine-level electrolyser facility. Turbine-level compression remains a separate downstream subsystem.

Physical and Economic Scope

The physical energy chain contains only equipment that captures, converts, collects, transports, or delivers energy. Installation and project-development activities are therefore not shown as energy-flow steps.

The economic boundary nevertheless includes the lifecycle activities required for every asset within the physical boundary:

  • procurement and fabrication
  • transport and installation
  • commissioning
  • operation, maintenance, and repair
  • component replacement where represented by the model
  • decommissioning where represented by the model

Common project costs remain inside the comparison when they are required by all architectures. They are labelled as common rather than removed from the system boundary. Wider network reinforcement beyond the defined connection point is excluded unless a scenario explicitly assigns it to the offshore project.
