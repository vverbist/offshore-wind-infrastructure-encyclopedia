Turbine System

Purpose and System Boundary

The turbine-system model calculates the mass and supply cost of one offshore wind turbine and its monopile foundation. It retains component masses because they connect turbine design to foundation sizing, transport, lifting, and installation-vessel requirements.

The model boundary includes the rotor, nacelle, turbine electrical equipment, tower, monopile, and a transition-piece supply allowance. It excludes offshore transport and installation, scour protection, array infrastructure, export infrastructure, and hydrogen-production equipment. Where architecture-specific equipment is supported by the turbine foundation, its mass is passed into the foundation calculation without moving its cost into the turbine-system boundary.

Calculation Dependency

The foundation model does not use turbine rated power directly. Rated power, rotor diameter, hub height, and electrical architecture first determine the turbine component ledger. The resulting complete turbine mass, including the tower, is then combined with any additional supported equipment and water depth to estimate monopile mass.

ImportantBook each item once

The turbine page owns the tower mass and cost. The foundation page uses the tower only through the complete turbine mass passed into its structural scaling; it does not add tower cost again. Likewise, the installation page consumes turbine and monopile masses but does not repeat their supply costs.

Architecture Applicability

The aerodynamic rotor and basic structural calculation are common to the electricity-export, centralised-hydrogen, and decentralised-hydrogen architectures. Their turbine electrical equipment can differ.

Architecture Turbine-system treatment
Electricity export Uses the conventional turbine electrical-component ledger
Centralised hydrogen Retains the turbine-side collection equipment required to deliver electricity to the central electrolyser
Decentralised hydrogen Removes or replaces electrical components according to the selected direct-DC arrangement

Architecture changes are made by changing explicit component masses and costs, not by applying a general percentage adjustment. Electrolyser, water-treatment, compression, storage, and hydrogen-export equipment remain owned by their respective pages. If any of that equipment is physically supported at the turbine, its mass enters \(m_\mathrm{additional}\) so that the foundation calculation responds to it.
