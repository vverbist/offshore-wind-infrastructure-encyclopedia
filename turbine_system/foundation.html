Monopile Foundation Mass and Cost

Role and Scope

This page estimates the fabricated mass and supply cost of one monopile foundation. The calculation is intended for early-stage comparison of fixed-bottom offshore wind concepts, before site-specific geotechnical and structural design is available.

The two model inputs are the total mass supported above the foundation and water depth. Turbine rated power is not an input: its structural consequence already enters through the component masses calculated on Wind Turbine Mass and Cost.

Included on this page Accounted elsewhere
Fabricated and coated monopile Turbine and tower supply
Transition-piece supply allowance Monopile and turbine installation
Mass scaling for concept comparison Scour protection
Detailed structural and geotechnical design

The cost boundary excludes transport, offshore installation, installation vessels, seabed preparation, scour protection, engineering, development, and operation and maintenance. Offshore installation is calculated on Turbine and Foundation Installation.

Model Inputs

Symbol Description Unit Status
\(m_\mathrm{top}\) Total supported mass above the foundation interface t Calculated upstream
\(d_w\) Water depth at the turbine location m Project input
\(c_\mathrm{MP}\) Monopile fabrication cost per tonne USD\(_{2022}\)/t Derived source range
\(r_\mathrm{TP}\) Transition-piece cost as a fraction of monopile cost – Sourced range

For the present system boundary,

\[ m_\mathrm{top}=m_\mathrm{turbine}+m_\mathrm{additional}, \]

where \(m_\mathrm{turbine}\) is the complete turbine mass, including the tower, passed from the turbine calculation. The optional \(m_\mathrm{additional}\) term includes equipment supported by the foundation but not already present in the turbine mass ledger. It is zero unless such equipment is modelled explicitly.

Monopile Mass

Monopile design is governed by environmental loads, soil conditions, natural-frequency constraints, fatigue, fabrication limits, water depth, and the mass and loading of the supported structure. Segeren and Diepeveen demonstrate that RNA mass affects monopile dimensions and structural response, but do not provide a universal mass-only sizing law (Segeren and Diepeveen 2014). A site-independent structural calculation is therefore not justified by the available inputs.

For screening, the model adopts the explicit scaling assumption

\[ m_\mathrm{MP} =m_\mathrm{MP,ref} \left(\frac{m_\mathrm{top}}{m_\mathrm{top,ref}}\right) \left(\frac{d_w}{d_\mathrm{ref}}\right), \]

where:

Symbol Description Unit
\(m_\mathrm{MP}\) Fabricated monopile mass t
\(m_\mathrm{MP,ref}\) Reference monopile mass t
\(m_\mathrm{top,ref}\) Reference supported mass t
\(d_\mathrm{ref}\) Reference water depth m

The reference is the IEA 15 MW offshore wind turbine (Gaertner et al. 2020):

Reference quantity Value Basis
RNA mass 1,017 t Published reference-turbine mass
Tower mass 860 t Published reference-turbine mass
\(m_\mathrm{top,ref}\) 1,877 t RNA plus tower
\(d_\mathrm{ref}\) 30 m Reference site water depth
\(m_\mathrm{MP,ref}\) 1,318 t Published reference monopile mass

Equivalently,

\[ m_\mathrm{MP}=k_m m_\mathrm{top}d_w, \]

with

\[ k_m =\frac{1{,}318}{1{,}877\times30} =0.02341\ \mathrm{m^{-1}}. \]

The direct proportionality to supported mass and water depth is a modelling assumption, not a structural-design equation. It is deliberately simple, passes through a traceable reference design, and avoids reintroducing turbine power as a proxy after turbine and tower masses have already been calculated.

Check against published designs

The scaling is checked against two independent design sources. For the IEA 22 MW reference turbine, the published RNA, tower, and monopile masses are 1,216 t, 1,574 t, and 2,097 t at a 34 m reference depth (Zahle et al. 2024). BVG Associates gives indicative monopile masses of 1,850 t at 40 m and 2,700 t at 60 m for a 15 MW turbine (BVG Associates 2025). Applying the IEA 15 MW supported mass to the BVG cases gives:

Comparison case \(m_\mathrm{top}\) \(d_w\) Reported \(m_\mathrm{MP}\) Model \(m_\mathrm{MP}\) Difference
IEA 22 MW reference 2,790 t 34 m 2,097 t 2,220 t +5.9%
BVG 15 MW example 1,877 t 40 m 1,850 t 1,757 t -5.0%
BVG 15 MW example 1,877 t 60 m 2,700 t 2,636 t -2.4%

Agreement within approximately 6% for these comparisons supports use as a screening relationship near large contemporary monopile designs. It does not establish validity outside that range or for unusual soil and load conditions.

Monopile Supply Cost

NREL reports a finished monopile cost range of USD\(_{2022}\) 250–350/kW for 12–20 MW turbines at 40 m water depth. The scope includes materials and fabrication labour but excludes transport and installation (Fuchs et al. 2024). NREL also reports that transition-piece cost is 50–75% of monopile cost.

Rated power is used only to convert this published cost range into a mass-based unit rate. It is not an input to the project calculation. Pairing NREL’s 15 MW case with BVG’s 1,850 t monopile at the same 40 m depth gives:

\[ c_\mathrm{MP,low} =\frac{15{,}000\times250}{1{,}850} =2{,}030\ \mathrm{USD_{2022}/t}, \]

\[ c_\mathrm{MP,high} =\frac{15{,}000\times350}{1{,}850} =2{,}840\ \mathrm{USD_{2022}/t}. \]

The midpoint is used as the baseline:

\[ c_\mathrm{MP,base}=2{,}430\ \mathrm{USD_{2022}/t}. \]

Monopile and transition-piece supply costs are then:

\[ C_\mathrm{MP}=m_\mathrm{MP}c_\mathrm{MP}, \]

\[ C_\mathrm{TP}=r_\mathrm{TP}C_\mathrm{MP}, \]

\[ C_\mathrm{foundation} =C_\mathrm{MP}+C_\mathrm{TP} =m_\mathrm{MP}c_\mathrm{MP}(1+r_\mathrm{TP}). \]

Case \(c_\mathrm{MP}\) \(r_\mathrm{TP}\)
Low 2,030 USD\(_{2022}\)/t 0.50
Baseline 2,430 USD\(_{2022}\)/t 0.625
High 2,840 USD\(_{2022}\)/t 0.75

The transition piece is represented as a cost allowance because the source reports a cost ratio rather than sufficient geometry for a separate material calculation. Its mass is not included in \(m_\mathrm{MP}\) and remains a project-specific input to transport and installation analysis.

Worked Example

The worked turbine calculation gives a known-component complete-turbine mass of 1,552.8 t. With no additional supported equipment and a water depth of 30 m:

\[ m_\mathrm{top}=1{,}552.8\ \mathrm{t}, \]

\[ m_\mathrm{MP} =1{,}318 \left(\frac{1{,}552.8}{1{,}877}\right) \left(\frac{30}{30}\right) =1{,}090\ \mathrm{t}. \]

The resulting supply-cost range is:

Case Monopile cost Transition-piece allowance Total foundation supply cost
Low USD\(_{2022}\) 2.21 million USD\(_{2022}\) 1.11 million USD\(_{2022}\) 3.32 million
Baseline USD\(_{2022}\) 2.65 million USD\(_{2022}\) 1.66 million USD\(_{2022}\) 4.31 million
High USD\(_{2022}\) 3.10 million USD\(_{2022}\) 2.32 million USD\(_{2022}\) 5.42 million

The values remain in the source’s 2022 USD basis. Currency conversion and escalation are applied later using the common financial assumptions.

Model Output and Limitations

The page passes two outputs to the wider model:

Output Downstream use
\(m_\mathrm{MP}\) Foundation fabrication cost and installation-vessel feasibility
\(C_\mathrm{foundation}\) Foundation supply CAPEX

The model is suitable for screening comparisons close to the cited 15–22 MW, 30–60 m reference range. It does not resolve soil profile, pile penetration, diameter, wall thickness, natural frequency, fatigue, corrosion allowance, secondary steel, grouted or bolted connections, or fabrication constraints. It must therefore be replaced by a site-specific integrated turbine, tower, and foundation design before detailed engineering or procurement.

No separate foundation OPEX percentage is applied. Foundation inspection and repair should enter the project OPEX model as explicit activities when suitable evidence is available.
