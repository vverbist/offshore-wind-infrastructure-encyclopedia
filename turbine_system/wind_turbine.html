Wind Turbine Mass and Cost

Role and Scope

This page calculates the component masses and supply cost of one offshore wind turbine. The parametric inputs are generator capacity \(P\), rotor diameter \(D\), and hub height \(H_h\). Component masses are retained individually because they determine structural loads, transport requirements, lift configurations, and installation-vessel feasibility.

The calculation uses the NREL Cost and Scaling Model (CSM) implemented in WISDEM. The CSM estimates component masses from high-level turbine inputs and then estimates component costs from those masses (National Renewable Energy Laboratory 2026a). It is used here because its equations and inputs are explicit. WISDEM notes that the regressions include older WindPACT-era relationships and recommends overriding them with more detailed estimates where available (National Renewable Energy Laboratory 2026a).

This implementation is pinned to WISDEM source revision 584c39191b61ab2654c1f1fab2871e0f5bec4064 (National Renewable Energy Laboratory 2026b). A different WISDEM revision must not be substituted without checking every coefficient below.

Included on this page Accounted elsewhere
Blades, hub, pitch system and spinner Monopile or other foundation
Mechanical drivetrain and generator Offshore turbine transport and installation
Nacelle structure and auxiliary systems Array and export cables
Turbine electrical equipment and transformer when present Offshore and onshore substations
Tower Grid connection and other balance-of-system costs

The cost boundary is turbine supply at the stated commercial basis. Offshore transport and installation are calculated on Turbine and Foundation Installation. For decentralised hydrogen, electrolyser, water-treatment, compression, storage, and hydrogen-export equipment are not turbine CAPEX. Their masses must nevertheless enter the installation and structural models when they are installed in or on the turbine.

Parametric Inputs

Symbol Description Unit
\(P\) Generator rated capacity kW
\(D\) Rotor diameter m
\(H_h\) Hub height above mean sea level m

When hub height is not specified independently, the default relationship provides 15 m lower-tip clearance above mean sea level:

\[ H_h=\frac{D}{2}+15. \]

The structural tower interface is assumed to be 15 m above mean sea level. Tower length is therefore derived rather than entered independently:

\[ L_t=H_h-15. \]

Under the default hub-height relationship, \(L_t=D/2\). A project-specific hub height can override the default while retaining the same 15 m interface elevation.

Fixed Model Assumptions

The following quantities are model constants or scenario definitions, not parametric inputs:

Quantity Baseline treatment
Number of blades \(n_b=3\)
Maximum blade-tip speed \(V_\mathrm{tip}=95\) m/s
Rated drivetrain efficiency \(\eta=0.95\)
Blade regression IEC Class I with carbon reinforcement
Main bearings \(n_\mathrm{brg}=2\)
Nacelle crane Included
Reference drivetrain Permanent-magnet direct drive
Electrical architecture Selected scenario: electricity export or decentralised hydrogen

Rated power and rotor diameter do not uniquely determine maximum blade-tip speed. Tip speed is a turbine design and control limit affected by aerodynamic, acoustic, structural, and generator constraints. Fixing it at the IEA 15 MW reference value keeps the parametric model limited to \(P\), \(D\), and \(H_h\) (Gaertner et al. 2020).

Calculation Sequence

The calculation is performed in dependency order:

  1. calculate rated rotor speed and torque;
  2. calculate blade and hub-system masses;
  3. calculate drivetrain, nacelle, electrical, and tower masses;
  4. select the components present in the chosen drivetrain and electrical architecture;
  5. aggregate rotor, nacelle, RNA, tower, and complete-turbine masses;
  6. calculate raw component costs; and
  7. calibrate the raw WISDEM cost to a stated offshore-turbine cost reference.

Rated Speed and Torque

The rated rotor angular velocity follows from the fixed 95 m/s tip-speed limit:

\[ \omega_r=\frac{V_\mathrm{tip}}{D/2}. \]

Rated rotor speed and low-speed-shaft torque are:

\[ n_r=\omega_r\frac{60}{2\pi}, \qquad Q_r=\frac{1000P/\eta}{\omega_r}, \]

where \(n_r\) is in revolutions per minute and \(Q_r\) is in Nm. A larger rotor rotates more slowly for the same tip-speed limit and consequently transmits more torque at the same rated power.

Rotor Mass

Blade

The baseline WISDEM mass of one blade is:

\[ m_b=0.5\left(\frac{D}{2}\right)^b, \]

where \(m_b\) is in kg. The exponent \(b\) depends on turbine class and carbon reinforcement (National Renewable Energy Laboratory 2026b).

Blade assumption \(b\)
Class I with carbon reinforcement 2.47
Class I without carbon reinforcement 2.54
Class II or III with carbon reinforcement 2.44
Class II or III without carbon reinforcement 2.50

The offshore screening case uses Class I with carbon reinforcement unless another class or blade design is specified.

Hub, pitch system, and spinner

Hub mass is:

\[ m_\mathrm{hub}=2.3m_b+1320. \]

WISDEM first calculates pitch-bearing mass and then complete pitch-system mass:

\[ m_\mathrm{pb}=0.1295n_bm_b+491.31, \]

\[ m_\mathrm{pitch}=1.328m_\mathrm{pb}+555. \]

Spinner mass is:

\[ m_\mathrm{spinner}=15.5D-980. \]

The hub-system and rotor masses are therefore:

\[ m_\mathrm{hubsys} =m_\mathrm{hub}+m_\mathrm{pitch}+m_\mathrm{spinner}, \]

\[ m_\mathrm{rotor}=n_bm_b+m_\mathrm{hubsys}. \]

The model retains blade, hub-system, and complete-rotor masses separately so that alternative rotor-assembly and lifting strategies can be evaluated.

Nacelle Mass

Low-speed shaft and main bearings

The low-speed-shaft mass is:

\[ m_\mathrm{LSS} =13\left(m_b\frac{P}{1000}\right)^{0.65}+775. \]

The mass of one main bearing and the total bearing mass are:

\[ m_\mathrm{bearing}=10^{-4}D^{3.5}, \qquad m_\mathrm{bearings}=n_\mathrm{brg}m_\mathrm{bearing}. \]

Gearbox, brake, and high-speed shaft

For a geared drivetrain, the pinned WISDEM source calculates gearbox mass from rated torque:

\[ m_\mathrm{gearbox}=\frac{Q_r}{\rho_Q}. \]

The source default is \(\rho_Q=200\) Nm/kg. Gearbox mass is zero for direct drive.

Brake and high-speed-shaft masses are:

\[ m_\mathrm{brake}=0.00122Q_r, \qquad m_\mathrm{HSS}=0.19894P. \]

The high-speed shaft is removed for direct drive. Brake mass must be reviewed for the selected direct-drive arrangement rather than retained automatically.

Generator

The simple WISDEM relationship is:

\[ m_\mathrm{generator}=2.3P+3400. \]

This power-based regression represents a relatively high-speed geared generator. It is not used for a large direct-drive offshore generator, whose mass is governed primarily by rated torque. The direct-drive replacement is defined under Improvements from Mehta.

Nacelle structure and auxiliaries

The remaining WISDEM mass relationships are:

\[ m_\mathrm{bedplate}=D^{2.2}, \]

\[ m_\mathrm{yaw}=1.5\left(0.0009D^{3.314}\right), \]

\[ m_\mathrm{HVAC}=0.08P, \]

\[ m_\mathrm{cover}=1.2817P+428.19, \]

\[ m_\mathrm{platforms} =0.125m_\mathrm{bedplate}+3000I_\mathrm{crane}, \]

\[ m_\mathrm{transformer}=1.915P+1910. \]

Transformer mass is included only when a turbine transformer is present in the selected architecture.

Electrical-equipment additions

The CSM does not provide adequate masses for converters, controls, switchgear, internal cabling, DC-link equipment, or electrical protection. Those terms are explicit architecture inputs:

\[ \begin{aligned} m_\mathrm{electrical}={}&m_\mathrm{converter,GS} +m_\mathrm{converter,grid} +m_\mathrm{switchgear}\\ &+m_\mathrm{cabling} +m_\mathrm{DC-link} +m_\mathrm{protection}, \end{aligned} \]

where GS denotes the generator-side converter. A missing required mass remains an unresolved input; it is not silently assigned a value of zero. The direct-DC electrical architecture is developed further on DC Integration.

Nacelle aggregation

Only components present in the selected drivetrain and electrical architecture are included:

\[ \begin{aligned} m_\mathrm{nacelle}={}&m_\mathrm{LSS}+m_\mathrm{bearings} +m_\mathrm{gearbox}+m_\mathrm{brake}+m_\mathrm{HSS}\\ &+m_\mathrm{generator}+m_\mathrm{bedplate}+m_\mathrm{yaw} +m_\mathrm{HVAC}+m_\mathrm{cover}\\ &+m_\mathrm{platforms}+m_\mathrm{transformer} +m_\mathrm{electrical}. \end{aligned} \]

Tower Mass

The simple WISDEM tower relationship is:

\[ m_\mathrm{tower}=19.828L_t^{2.0282}. \]

This regression depends only on tower length. It does not respond to rotor thrust, RNA mass, tower natural-frequency constraints, foundation stiffness, fatigue, or site conditions. It is therefore an initial screening estimate, not the preferred offshore-tower calculation.

TowerSE or an equivalent load-based calculation is required before tower-section masses are used for detailed installation planning. The tower remains inside the turbine-cost boundary; the foundation remains outside it.

Aggregate Masses and Installation Outputs

Rotor-nacelle assembly and complete-turbine masses are:

\[ m_\mathrm{RNA}=m_\mathrm{rotor}+m_\mathrm{nacelle}, \]

\[ m_\mathrm{turbine}=m_\mathrm{RNA}+m_\mathrm{tower}. \]

The calculation passes the following outputs to the installation model:

Output Installation use
Individual blade mass and blade length Blade transport, racks, and lifting
Hub-system and complete-rotor mass Hub or preassembled-rotor lift
Nacelle mass Nacelle lift
RNA mass Structural loading and alternative lift strategies
Tower mass and, when available, section masses Tower transport and lifts
Largest dimensions and heaviest lift Vessel and crane feasibility

Tower-section masses and dimensions are not generated by the simple CSM. Until TowerSE is implemented, those installation inputs remain unresolved.

Component Cost

The WISDEM CSM generally calculates raw manufacturing cost as:

\[ C_i=u_im_i, \]

where \(u_i\) is a component mass-cost coefficient. Electrical connections and controls instead scale with rated capacity. The pinned source uses the following coefficients (National Renewable Energy Laboratory 2026b):

The coefficients are legacy model inputs with mixed evidential ages. They are used to distribute and scale cost between components, not as a current market quotation.

Raw subsystem costs are:

\[ C_\mathrm{rotor,raw} =n_bC_b+C_\mathrm{hub}+C_\mathrm{pitch}+C_\mathrm{spinner}, \]

\[ C_\mathrm{nacelle,raw}=\sum_{i\in\mathrm{nacelle}}C_i, \]

\[ C_\mathrm{turbine,raw} =C_\mathrm{rotor,raw}+C_\mathrm{nacelle,raw}+C_\mathrm{tower,raw}. \]

WISDEM permits assembly, overhead, profit, and transport multipliers. Their defaults are zero (National Renewable Energy Laboratory 2026a). Offshore transport remains excluded on this page. Assembly, overhead, profit, and warranty must either be stated as separate supply-cost adders or included through the calibration below.

Cost Calibration

The raw WISDEM costs are not used as the absolute turbine price. Separate RNA and tower calibration factors preserve WISDEM’s component scaling while matching a stated NREL offshore reference:

\[ k_\mathrm{RNA} =\frac{C_\mathrm{RNA,ref}^{\mathrm{NREL}}} {C_\mathrm{RNA,ref}^{\mathrm{WISDEM}}}, \qquad k_\mathrm{tower} =\frac{C_\mathrm{tower,ref}^{\mathrm{NREL}}} {C_\mathrm{tower,ref}^{\mathrm{WISDEM}}}. \]

The calibrated turbine supply cost is:

\[ C_\mathrm{turbine} =k_\mathrm{RNA}C_\mathrm{RNA,raw} +k_\mathrm{tower}C_\mathrm{tower,raw}. \]

The current calibration candidate is NREL’s 2022 Cost of Wind Energy Review. Its offshore reference is a 12 MW turbine with a 216 m rotor and 137 m hub height. It reports turbine CAPEX of USD 1,700/kW, separated into USD 1,462/kW for the RNA and USD 238/kW for the tower; installation is a separate cost category (Stehly et al. 2023). These values define a reference point, not a linear EUR/kW turbine model.

Currency conversion and escalation are applied after calibration using the common financial assumptions. The source currency, source year, index, and exchange-rate convention must accompany every reported result.

Decentralised Hydrogen Architecture

The decentralised-hydrogen turbine is represented by changing the component ledger. No general percentage reduction is applied to turbine mass or cost.

For architecture \(a\):

\[ m_{\mathrm{nacelle},a} =\sum_i x_{i,a}m_i+\sum_jm_{j,a}^{\mathrm{replacement}}, \]

\[ C_{\mathrm{turbine},a} =\sum_i x_{i,a}C_i+\sum_jC_{j,a}^{\mathrm{replacement}}, \]

where \(x_{i,a}=1\) when component \(i\) is present and zero when it is absent.

A conventional electricity-export turbine may contain a generator-side converter or rectifier, DC link, grid-side inverter, step-up transformer, AC switchgear, protection, and high-voltage internal cabling. A direct-DC hydrogen turbine may remove or resize the grid-side inverter, transformer, part of the AC switchgear, and part of the high-voltage cabling. It may require replacement DC-link, DC protection, DC-DC conversion, cooling, and structural equipment.

The mass and cost changes are therefore:

\[ \Delta m_\mathrm{H2} =m_\mathrm{removed}-m_\mathrm{replacement}+m_\mathrm{reinforcement}, \]

\[ \Delta C_\mathrm{H2} =C_\mathrm{removed}-C_\mathrm{replacement}+C_\mathrm{reinforcement}. \]

WISDEM calculates transformer mass explicitly. Converter, switchgear, protection, and cabling masses require separate evidence. A lighter nacelle may also reduce tower and foundation mass, but that secondary effect cannot be calculated with the length-only WISDEM tower regression.

Improvements from Mehta

Mehta, Zaaijer, and von Terzi scale turbine properties around the direct-drive IEA 15 MW reference turbine (Mehta et al. 2024; Gaertner et al. 2020). Their complete model is not substituted for WISDEM: the paper does not publish standalone equations for every component, and its absolute turbine-cost calibration is not adopted. Four relationships nevertheless improve identifiable weaknesses in the WISDEM baseline.

1. Load-responsive blade mass

WISDEM blade mass depends on \(D\) but does not respond to the higher rated wind speed and thrust associated with greater specific power. Mehta introduces:

\[ m_b =m_{b,\mathrm{ref}} \left(\frac{D}{D_\mathrm{ref}}\right)^\beta \left(\frac{V_r}{V_{r,\mathrm{ref}}}\right)^2, \]

with baseline \(\beta=3\). Rated wind speed is calculated from:

\[ V_r =\left( \frac{1000P} {\tfrac{1}{2}\rho A C_{P,\max}\eta} \right)^{1/3}, \qquad A=\frac{\pi D^2}{4}. \]

This relationship should replace the WISDEM blade-mass regression when the model varies both \(P\) and \(D\) around the IEA 15 MW design. Hub, pitch, and spinner masses remain separate WISDEM components. The exponent \(\beta\) is an uncertainty input because geometric cubic scaling does not represent every future blade technology.

2. Mixed blade-cost scaling

WISDEM makes blade cost proportional to blade mass. Mehta separates material-related cost from tooling, labour, and other diameter-related costs:

\[ C_b =\gamma_\mathrm{mass}C_{b,\mathrm{ref}} \frac{m_b}{m_{b,\mathrm{ref}}} +(1-\gamma_\mathrm{mass})C_{b,\mathrm{ref}} \left(\frac{D}{D_\mathrm{ref}}\right)^\alpha. \]

The baseline parameters are \(\gamma_\mathrm{mass}=0.6\) and \(\alpha=2\) (Mehta et al. 2024). This replaces only the blade cost relationship. The absolute reference blade cost must come from the adopted NREL-compatible calibration; Mehta’s absolute reference cost is not used.

3. Torque-based direct-drive generator

For a permanent-magnet direct-drive turbine, generator mass is scaled with rated torque:

\[ m_\mathrm{generator} =m_\mathrm{generator,ref} \frac{Q_r}{Q_{r,\mathrm{ref}}}. \]

The archived Mehta implementation uses \(m_\mathrm{generator,ref}=372{,}000\) kg and \(Q_{r,\mathrm{ref}}=20.308\) MNm (Mehta 2023). This corresponds to a reference torque density of approximately 54.6 Nm/kg. The relationship replaces the WISDEM power-based generator equation for direct drive. Generator torque density remains an uncertainty parameter because electromagnetic architecture, structural support, and cooling can change it materially.

4. Tower feedback

Mehta scales hub height linearly from the IEA 15 MW reference. That relationship is not adopted here because the model instead uses the directly interpretable lower-tip-clearance relationship \(H_h=D/2+15\). A project-specific hub height can override this default.

Mehta’s support-structure method nevertheless demonstrates that tower mass must respond to RNA mass, rotor thrust, hub height, and site conditions. The full coupled monopile calculation is outside the turbine boundary, so the intended improvement is TowerSE rather than importing Mehta’s complete support-structure module.

Model Status and Limitations

The implemented hierarchy is:

  1. WISDEM provides the component ledger and baseline mass equations.
  2. Mehta replaces blade mass and blade cost when both capacity and rotor diameter vary.
  3. Mehta’s torque relationship replaces the WISDEM generator equation for direct drive.
  4. Architecture-specific component inputs represent conventional electricity export and decentralised hydrogen.
  5. Separate RNA and tower factors calibrate raw WISDEM costs to NREL.
  6. All component masses pass to the later installation calculation.

The calculation remains incomplete for detailed design until converter, switchgear, internal-cabling, and DC-interface masses are sourced and a load-based tower calculation produces tower-section geometry. These missing inputs are exposed explicitly. The model is a transparent scaling model, not a certified turbine design or supplier quotation, and is most reliable near the scale and architecture of its reference turbine.

Worked Example: 15 MW Turbine with a 236 m Rotor

This example evaluates a conventional electricity-export turbine with:

\[ P=15{,}000\ \mathrm{kW}, \qquad D=236\ \mathrm{m}. \]

The default hub-height and tower-length relationships give:

\[ H_h=\frac{236}{2}+15=133\ \mathrm{m}, \]

\[ L_t=H_h-15=118\ \mathrm{m}. \]

The fixed assumptions are three blades, 95 m/s maximum tip speed, 95% drivetrain efficiency, two main bearings, a nacelle crane, and a permanent-magnet direct-drive generator. Mehta’s load-responsive relationship is used for blade mass and its torque relationship is used for generator mass. The remaining applicable component masses follow the pinned WISDEM equations.

Operating point

Rated rotor speed and torque are:

\[ n_r=7.69\ \mathrm{rpm}, \qquad Q_r=19.61\ \mathrm{MNm}. \]

Cost calibration

The raw WISDEM component costs are calibrated to NREL’s 12 MW offshore reference. The NREL reference uses \(P_\mathrm{ref}=12\) MW, \(D_\mathrm{ref}=216\) m, \(H_{h,\mathrm{ref}}=137\) m, RNA cost of USD 1,462/kW, and tower cost of USD 238/kW (Stehly et al. 2023).

The raw blade-cost anchor is the WISDEM coefficient applied to the 65 t IEA 15 MW reference blade:

\[ C_{b,\mathrm{ref,raw}} =14.6\times65{,}000 =949{,}000\ \mathrm{USD/blade}. \]

This anchor is used in Mehta’s mixed mass/non-mass blade-cost relationship for both the NREL calibration turbine and the 236 m example. The subsequent RNA calibration, rather than the raw blade anchor, sets the absolute turbine cost level.

At the NREL reference geometry, the model gives raw RNA and tower costs of USD 10.232 million and USD 0.980 million. The published calibration totals are:

\[ C_\mathrm{RNA,ref}^{\mathrm{NREL}} =12{,}000\times1{,}462 =17.544\ \mathrm{million\ USD}, \]

\[ C_\mathrm{tower,ref}^{\mathrm{NREL}} =12{,}000\times238 =2.856\ \mathrm{million\ USD}. \]

The resulting factors are:

\[ k_\mathrm{RNA}=1.7146, \qquad k_\mathrm{tower}=2.9142. \]

The RNA factor is applied to every raw rotor and nacelle cost. The tower factor is applied only to the raw tower cost. This preserves the component-level WISDEM scaling while reproducing the separate NREL RNA and tower reference costs.
