Turbine Power Electronics

Role and Accounting Boundary

This page defines the turbine electrical output used to compare electricity export, centralised hydrogen and decentralised hydrogen. The adopted model boundary is the AC output of the grid-side inverter, before the step-up transformer. The generator and generator-side rectifier remain common to all cases; their costs and losses are not removed when comparing architectures.

The transformer is outside the turbine cost and power-output boundary used here. It is usually housed inside the turbine, in the tower or nacelle, but physical location does not determine cost ownership (BVG Associates 2019). Its purchase cost and downstream losses belong to electrical infrastructure; its mass still belongs in the applicable supported-mass and lifting inventory.

Single-Line Diagram

The DC branch replaces the AC path; it is not an additional simultaneous export path. “LV side” identifies the lower-voltage transformer winding relative to the infield grid; it does not prescribe a particular equipment voltage class.

Power Available to Each Architecture

Required inputs are the turbine power curve at the declared boundary and the inverter and transformer efficiencies. Their specifications and evidence belong to Power Conversion Equipment. No numerical efficiency is independently defined on this page.

For electricity export and centralised hydrogen, the power delivered to the infield grid is:

\[ P_{\mathrm{array,in}}=P_{\mathrm{curve}}\eta_{\mathrm{tr}}. \]

For decentralised hydrogen, removing the inverter recovers its conversion loss:

\[ P_{\mathrm{DC}}=\frac{P_{\mathrm{curve}}}{\eta_{\mathrm{inv}}}, \qquad \Delta P_{\mathrm{inv}}=P_{\mathrm{curve}} \left(\frac{1}{\eta_{\mathrm{inv}}}-1\right). \]

Here \(P_{\mathrm{curve}}\) is inverter AC output, \(P_{\mathrm{array,in}}\) is transformer output, \(P_{\mathrm{DC}}\) is available DC-link power and \(\Delta P_{\mathrm{inv}}\) is recovered inverter loss, all in kW. \(\eta_{\mathrm{inv}}\) and \(\eta_{\mathrm{tr}}\) are dimensionless output/input efficiencies at the applicable operating point. Division reverses the inverter loss; adding a loss percentage is not equivalent. These expressions assume the same upstream operating point and do not authorise operation beyond the generator, rectifier or DC interface ratings.

The DC Integration page defines the direct connection of the electrolyser to the DC link and its matching and protection conditions. The conservative alternative retains a dedicated stack-side DC/DC converter. In either case, retained interface losses are applied after recovering inverter losses. Neither architecture receives a generator or rectifier efficiency credit.

Cost Boundary

For comparable per-turbine supply costs in EUR2025, let \(C_{\mathrm{legacy}}\) denote a conventional turbine package that includes the transformer, \(C_{\mathrm{tr}}\) its consistently allocated transformer cost, and \(C_{\mathrm{inv}}\) the separable grid-side inverter cost. Then:

\[ C_{\mathrm{turbine,AC}}=C_{\mathrm{legacy}}-C_{\mathrm{tr}}, \qquad C_{\mathrm{turbine,DC}}=C_{\mathrm{turbine,AC}}-C_{\mathrm{inv}}. \]

The AC architectures book \(C_{\mathrm{tr}}\) once under electrical infrastructure. The decentralised architecture does not purchase it. Added DC/DC equipment and changes to protection, cooling and cabling are separate retained costs, not part of the inverter credit. An unknown inverter price is not a zero price.

The turbine cost ledger currently includes the transformer in its calibrated conventional package. Reallocation must use that same calibrated term and preserve the combined AC supply cost; subtracting an unrelated supplier benchmark would change the calibration.

Reference-Curve and Implementation Status

The inverter-output boundary above is the adopted model convention, not a claim that every published turbine curve uses the same measurement point. A curve must identify whether converter and transformer losses are already included.
