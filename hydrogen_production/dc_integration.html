DC Integration

Direct integration of PEM electrolyser on the DC link of wind turbine

Role in the Model

The decentralised-hydrogen architecture directly couples a wind-turbine generator to a PEM electrolyser through a shared DC system. The model uses this configuration to remove conversion equipment that is only required for AC export or for supplying a conventional AC-fed electrolyser. The feasibility basis is the Hygro technical assessment of direct DC PEM-wind coupling (Verbist and Damen 2026).

The modelled electrical chain is:

wind-turbine generator -> active rectifier/up-chopper -> DC link -> PEM stack or stack array

The architecture has no AC export connection, turbine grid-side inverter, electrolyser rectifier, or separate electrolyser-side DC/DC converter. The generator-side active rectifier/up-chopper remains part of the turbine and controls generator current, electromagnetic torque, and the power extracted from the rotor.

NoteModel assumption

A dedicated electrolyser-side DC/DC converter is not treated as a fundamental requirement. Its functions are instead allocated to generator power control, generator-stack electrical matching, supervisory operating limits, and coordinated protection. This is an integrated turbine-electrolyser design assumption, not an off-the-shelf equipment configuration.

Operating Principle

The generator-side converter controls the electrical power supplied to the PEM system. The PEM polarisation characteristic then establishes the corresponding DC voltage and current. For a single stack, or the equivalent polarisation curve of the active stack array, the operating point satisfies:

\[ P_{\mathrm{gen}} = V_{\mathrm{stack}}\!\left(I_{\mathrm{stack}}\right) I_{\mathrm{stack}}. \]

where:

  • \(P_{\mathrm{gen}}\) is the generator electrical power supplied to the DC system, in W;
  • \(I_{\mathrm{stack}}\) is the total current through the active stack configuration, in A; and
  • \(V_{\mathrm{stack}}(I_{\mathrm{stack}})\) is the voltage given by its polarisation curve at the applicable temperature, pressure, hydration state, tolerance, and age, in V.

As generator power rises or falls, the operating point moves along the applicable polarisation curve. The DC-link voltage is therefore an operating result rather than the primary controlled variable. Voltage is still measured for operating-envelope supervision, converter limits, and protection, but normal hydrogen production does not require a fixed DC-voltage reference.

This distinction replaces the previous page assumption that an active-front-end voltage loop must hold the DC link at a prescribed value. Even where an active rectifier is used, its normal control objective in this architecture is generator current, torque, and power.

Model Calculation

At each operating point, the turbine power command is bounded by the available wind power, generator capability, and the power that the PEM system can safely accept:

\[ P_{\mathrm{cmd}} = \min\!\left( P_{\mathrm{wind,available}}, P_{\mathrm{gen,max}}, P_{\mathrm{PEM,permitted}} \right). \]

All four terms are powers in W. The permitted PEM power is a supervisory limit derived from current density, stack temperature and pressure, gas purity, water supply, cooling capacity, and the active stack configuration.

The electrical operating point is then found by solving:

\[ P_{\mathrm{cmd}} = V_{\mathrm{stack}}\!\left(I_{\mathrm{stack}}\right) I_{\mathrm{stack}} \]

within the permitted current and voltage range. The resulting \(I_{\mathrm{stack}}\) and \(V_{\mathrm{stack}}\) are passed to the PEM stack model to calculate hydrogen production, efficiency, and degradation.

If a modular stack array is used, branch switching changes the equivalent polarisation curve and provides coarse capacity selection. Generator torque remains the continuous power actuator.

Allocation of Converter Functions

Removing the separate stack converter does not remove its required functions. It reallocates them across the integrated system.

Allocation of functions normally associated with a separate electrolyser-side converter.
Function Provision in the direct-DC architecture
Power and current control Generator torque and current control, bounded by the power that the active PEM configuration can safely accept
Voltage adaptation Co-design of generator winding voltage, generator speed range, rectifier/up-chopper range, and PEM cell count
PEM operating limits Supervisory permitted-power envelope based on current density, temperature, pressure, gas purity, water, and cooling conditions
Protection and isolation Coordinated turbine and electrolyser protection; earthing, insulation, and any galvanic-isolation requirement remain explicit system-design inputs

Feasibility Conditions

Electrical Matching

The full PEM polarisation envelope must remain within the generator-side voltage and current ratings. The check must cover generator speed, stack temperature and pressure, manufacturing tolerance, and ageing. Matching is achieved through the generator winding design, converter range, series cell count, and any stack-array series/parallel configuration.

Boost-Only Constraint

If the generator-side power stage can only raise voltage, the passive rectified generator voltage must remain below the required stack voltage across the permitted speed range. A design that violates this condition would require a buck-capable conversion stage or a different generator-stack match.

Coordinated Control

The PEM controller must provide a continuously updated permitted-power limit. The turbine controller then applies the lowest of available wind power, generator capability, and this PEM limit. Communication failure must lead to a defined safe state rather than continued operation on a stale limit.

Modular Stack Option

A modular array may improve low-load operation, availability, and maintainability. Branch switching can select the active capacity in discrete steps, while generator torque control adjusts power continuously. Modularity is beneficial but is not a prerequisite for the underlying direct-coupling principle.

Start, Stop, and Faults

Turbine current reduction, pitch control, braking, and electrical isolation must be coordinated with PEM start permissives, stack isolation, purge, and safe shutdown. The control design must define timing and authority for start-up, normal stopping, load rejection, communication loss, and emergency shutdown.

Harmonics and Drivetrain Effects

Diode rectification can introduce generator-current harmonics and electromagnetic torque ripple. Generator electromagnetic design, converter thermal loading, and drivetrain loads therefore require validation for the selected topology (Andresen et al. 2025).

WarningUnresolved design input

The repository does not yet provide the project-specific generator winding voltage, speed envelope, rectifier/up-chopper limits, or PEM polarisation envelopes over temperature, pressure, tolerance, and ageing. Until those inputs are available, the model can define the operating-point calculation and equipment boundary but cannot report a verified matched operating envelope.

Evidence Base

Direct Proof of Principle

Clarke et al. directly coupled a 2.4 kW photovoltaic array to a PEM electrolyser without an intervening MPPT unit or DC/DC converter. Series-parallel configurations were selected to match the source and electrolyser voltage-current characteristics, and the system accumulated approximately 600 operating hours. This demonstrates that deliberate electrical matching and the PEM polarisation characteristic can establish a stable operating point without an intervening converter (Clarke et al. 2009).

This is proof of the electrical principle, not direct evidence for a multi-megawatt wind turbine.

Wind-Specific Proof of Principle

Chantharasenawong et al. investigated direct coupling of a fixed-pitch, variable-speed aerogenerator to PEM electrolysers without intervening voltage-conversion or maximum-power-point-tracking electronics. The remote-area system is not representative of a modern offshore turbine, but it directly supports matching a wind generator and PEM load (Chantharasenawong et al. 2008).

Industrial Architecture Support

ITM Power and Ørsted proposed placing an electrolyser at an individual Type IV wind turbine and connecting it directly to the turbine DC link. They identify fewer conversion steps, lower losses, a smaller electrolyser footprint, and removal of AC rectification as potential benefits. The publication also states that the system had not been demonstrated in practice, so it supports the intended architecture but does not prove this exact converter arrangement at scale (ITM Power and Ørsted 2020).

Adjacent Electrochemical Precedent

Fraunhofer ICT’s RedoxWind project connects a redox-flow battery directly to the intermediate DC circuit of a 2 MW wind turbine, eliminating an additional conversion step. A battery is not electrically or operationally identical to a PEM electrolyser, but the project validates turbine-level integration of an electrochemical system on a shared DC circuit (Fraunhofer Institute for Chemical Technology ICT, n.d.).

Engineering Qualification

Recent DC wind-turbine research confirms the potential benefit of fewer conversion stages but generally retains high-power DC/DC conversion for voltage adaptation, isolation, and fault handling. This shows that a DC/DC converter may be useful; it does not show that one is inherently required to determine the PEM operating point. The same work identifies current harmonics and torque ripple as validation topics for diode-rectifier concepts (Andresen et al. 2025).

Validation Priorities

Integration-specific validation priorities.
Validation area Required demonstration
Operating envelope Verify the matched generator and PEM voltage-current range over speed, temperature, pressure, tolerance, and ageing
Control coordination Demonstrate generator-power control against the PEM polarisation model, including low-load transitions and branch switching where used
Faults and sequencing Validate start permission, load rejection, communication failure, isolation, purge, and safe shutdown with agreed timing and authority
Electrical and mechanical effects Measure rectifier harmonics, torque ripple, thermal loading, and current sharing before progressing to a multi-megawatt demonstrator

Component-level turbine and PEM qualification remains necessary but is not repeated here. The new work is the matching, exchange of limits, timing, and allocation of control authority across the shared interface.

Model Implication

For the decentralised-hydrogen architecture, the model therefore:

  • retains the generator-side active rectifier/up-chopper and its losses;
  • excludes the turbine grid-side inverter and AC export equipment from the hydrogen power path;
  • excludes an electrolyser rectifier and separate electrolyser-side DC/DC converter;
  • determines stack voltage and current from generator power and the active PEM polarisation curve; and
  • retains supervisory controls, measurement, protection, isolation, and balance-of-plant interlocks as required functions.

No converter CAPEX or conversion-loss allowance should be assigned to a separate electrolyser-side DC/DC stage in this architecture. This treatment applies only if the project-specific electrical matching and protection requirements can be met.

ImportantFinal assessment

Direct DC coupling of a wind-turbine generator and PEM electrolyser without a dedicated electrolyser-side DC/DC converter is technically credible. The turbine controls generator power, while the PEM polarisation characteristic establishes the corresponding DC voltage and current. The remaining gap is full-scale engineering validation, not proof of the underlying electrical principle.
