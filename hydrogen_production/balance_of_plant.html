Balance of Plant

Role and Scope

The balance of plant (BOP) includes the non-electrical auxiliary equipment required to operate the PEM electrolyser stack safely and continuously. In this model, the BOP manages feedwater supply, heat removal, gas-liquid separation, hydrogen drying, hydrogen-side piping, instrumentation and housing.

Because the system is offshore, the feedwater system must also include seawater intake, desalination and final purification to electrolyser-grade water.

Power electronics are excluded completely because the required components depend on the electrical architecture. Transformers, rectifiers and other electrical conversion equipment are discussed on Electrolyser Power Electronics.

The stack, compressors, hydrogen export system, offshore installation, offshore structures and indirect project costs are also represented elsewhere in the model.

The scope used in this page is therefore:

Scope boundary for the balance-of-plant model.
Included in this BOP model Excluded from this BOP model
Thermal management PEM electrolyser stack
Feedwater handling Power electronics
Seawater intake Transformers and rectifiers
Desalination and final purification Hydrogen compression
Gas-liquid separation Hydrogen export system
Hydrogen drying Offshore foundations and structures
Hydrogen-side BOP piping Offshore installation
Instrumentation and housing Site preparation and indirect costs

Reference purchase cost

The model returns one aggregate BOP equipment purchase cost in EUR2025. Internal stack-water handling is included in that aggregate; desalination is not a separate cost-ledger item. Installation, platforms, compression, stack purchase and power electronics remain outside this boundary.

Water supply is retained in full. It includes feedwater treatment, the feed tank, polishing, filtration and recirculation equipment (Badgett et al. 2024, sec. 2.2.1 and Table 3). The DEA increment is interpreted as thermal seawater desalination upstream of this existing water-handling scope. No extra internal water-handling charge or cooling credit is applied. This is a screening boundary: the supplier breakdown is insufficient to verify all heat-exchanger and purification interfaces, so overlap must be checked when package evidence improves.

For source rating \(P_0\) [kW], summed manufactured unit cost \(c_0\) [USD2020/kW], markup multiplier \(M\) [-], USD escalation \(I_\mathrm{US}\) [-] and exchange rate \(X\) [USD/EUR], the source purchase cost is

\[ C_{0,2025}=c_0P_0M I_\mathrm{US}/X. \]

The common price method supplies \(I_\mathrm{US}\) and \(X\). The calculated source purchase cost is 435,926 EUR2025.

Scale this existing BOP to the adopted reference rating \(P_r\) [kW] using the reference-building exponent \(\alpha_r\) [-]:

\[ C_{\mathrm{process},r}=C_{0,2025}(P_r/P_0)^{\alpha_r}. \]

This gives 3,322,623 EUR2025. The source category costs are not multiplied linearly by the larger reference rating.

DNV’s thermal treatment coefficient \(u_w\) [EUR2023/MW] anchors the additional allowance at the same rating:

\[ C_{w,r,2023}=u_w(P_r/1000),\qquad C_{w,r,2025}=C_{w,r,2023}(1+h_{2024})(1+h_{2025}). \]

The division by 1000 converts kW to MW. The source-basis allowance is 195,000 EUR2023. DNV does not establish the quote’s price year. Treating it as the report-year EUR2023 is an adopted screening exception, not sourced price-year evidence. The euro-area annual HICP rates \(h_{2024}\) = 2.4% and \(h_{2025}\) = 2.1% are reused from the shared input table (Eurostat 2026). Consumer inflation is a fallback rather than an equipment-specific index. The derived escalation factor is 1.045504, giving 203,873 EUR2025. No manufacturer markup is added to the DEA supplier allowance, and its separately listed installation percentage is not added to purchase cost.

Finally,

\[ C_{\mathrm{BOP},r}=C_{\mathrm{process},r}+C_{w,r,2025}. \]

The aggregate reference is 3,526,497 EUR2025. These contributions explain the reference; the model books their sum once as BOP.

Scaling method

For wind capacity served at one production location \(P\) [kW], maximum block rating \(P_{\max}\) [kW], block count \(n_b\) [-] and block rating \(P_i\) [kW]:

\[ n_b=\left\lceil P/P_{\max}\right\rceil,\qquad P_i=P/n_b,\qquad C_{\mathrm{BOP,location}}=n_b C_{\mathrm{BOP},r}(P_i/P_r)^\alpha. \]

Sum these costs across production locations. Centralised production shares farm capacity across blocks; distributed production sizes blocks at each turbine. Stack overplanting does not resize BOP. No spare block is included.

The baseline exponent \(\alpha\) is 0.75. DNV proposes this exponent for BOP, while listing thermal treatment separately (DNV 2023, sec. 3.2.1 and 3.3). Applying it to the entire aggregate, including water treatment, is the adopted modelling assumption. The DEA linear allowance is used only to establish the reference, not reapplied at each block rating.

Retain 0.6 as an explicit sensitivity override of bop-scaling-exponent. The reference-building exponent remains unchanged, preserving the same reference cost in both cases. Scenario result input records retain the baseline and applied override. This tests economies of scale without changing the turbine-scale anchor.

BOP Power Consumption

The BOP also consumes electricity during electrolyser operation. This consumption is treated separately from stack electricity consumption.

The BOP power consumption represents parasitic loads from auxiliary systems such as pumps, fans, chillers, recirculation blowers, thermal management and hydrogen drying.

Compression is excluded from this value because compression is represented elsewhere in the model. Electrical conversion losses are also explicitly excluded because power electronics are represented separately.

The NREL report cites a generic BOP electricity consumption of 4.2 kWh/kg H₂ (Table 1), but this value includes electrical losses and is therefore not directly applicable to the non-electrical BOP scope used here (Badgett et al. 2024).

The underlying Ruth and Ahluwalia source (Badgett et al. 2024) separates BOP losses into electrical, mechanical and hydrogen-related losses. After excluding electrical losses, the remaining BOP consumption is:

Non-electrical BOP electricity consumption after excluding electrical losses.
Operating point Mechanical [kWh/kg H₂] Hydrogen [kWh/kg H₂] Total [kWh/kg H₂]
Rated power, BOL 1.3 0.2 1.5
Rated power, EOL 2.3 0.2 2.5
Minimum-loss point, BOL 0.4 0.1 0.5
Minimum-loss point, EOL 0.6 0.1 0.7

A simple lifetime average gives approximately 2.0 kWh/kg H₂ at rated power and 0.6 kWh/kg H₂ at the minimum-loss operating point. The difference is mainly caused by thermal management. At the minimum-loss operating point, the stack operates more efficiently and therefore produces less waste heat. Lower waste heat reduces the cooling duty and associated mechanical power consumption.

In this study, the stack is deliberately oversized so that the electrolyser usually operates closer to the minimum-loss point than to rated stack operation. The model therefore assumes an average non-electrical BOP power consumption of:

\[ e_\mathrm{BOP} = 1.0 \ \mathrm{kWh/kg_{H_2}} \]

This value includes mechanical BOP loads and hydrogen drying losses. It is selected as a conservative average. It is higher than the minimum-loss values reported by Ruth and Ahluwalia to allow for offshore cooling, water handling, drying, control systems, part-load operation and uncertainty in auxiliary loads.

Water-treatment integration

The adopted DEA cost evidence assumes thermal desalination using electrolyser waste heat (DNV 2023, sec. 3.3). The existing aggregate BOP electricity allowance is retained; no separate desalination electrical load is added. This is not a verified thermal balance. TODO: establish usable heat at the operating temperatures and part-load states of the oversized stack, treatment duty and buffering, and check the aggregate auxiliary allowance. Failure to provide sufficient heat would require a different treatment design and cost basis, rather than assuming free supplementary heat.

The total BOP electricity consumption is calculated as:

\[ E_\mathrm{BOP} = e_\mathrm{BOP} \cdot m_\mathrm{H_2} \]

where \(E_\mathrm{BOP}\) is the BOP electricity consumption and \(m_\mathrm{H_2}\) is the hydrogen production.

The assumed value of \(e_\mathrm{BOP}\) should be interpreted as an average value for an oversized offshore PEM electrolyser operating predominantly away from rated stack power.

Model limitations

  • The NREL source describes an onshore modular system. Scaling it to the adopted reference and then to shared blocks is a screening approximation.
  • Internal water handling remains within BOP. The DEA increment assumes upstream thermal desalination; supplier evidence must resolve purification and cooling interfaces before claiming a fully reconciled equipment inventory.
  • Thermal integration and auxiliary consumption require validation at the actual stack operating conditions. The cost allowance does not demonstrate feasibility.
  • The DEA quote year is assumed, and HICP is a broad price-index proxy.
  • Aggregate scaling omits treatment-package thresholds and redundancy. The retained block limit bounds centralisation benefits but is not engineering qualification.
  • Offshore intake scope, integration and installation remain unresolved; no zero costs are inferred. Offshore installation belongs to its owning campaign model.

The workflow exposes physical.bop_purchase_cost_basis for auditing the source subtotal, reference contributions, price conversion and aggregate reference.
