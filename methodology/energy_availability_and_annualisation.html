Energy and Availability

Role in the Model

This page defines the delivered-energy basis and combines annual availability once for each architecture. Component pages own efficiencies, auxiliary demand and subsystem availability evidence. Financial annualization is owned by Financial and Price-Basis Assumptions.

Energy Basis

Electricity is reported as electrical energy landed at the defined onshore electrical delivery point.

Hydrogen is reported as landed mass and HHV energy. The adopted rounded higher heating value is:

Parameter Symbol Value Basis
Hydrogen higher heating value \(HHV_{\mathrm{H2}}\) 39.4 kWh/kg Rounded from 39.42 kWh/kg; liquid water as combustion product (Independent Review Panel 2009)

For landed hydrogen mass \(m_{\mathrm{H2}}\) in kg/year:

\[ E_{\mathrm{H2,HHV}} = m_{\mathrm{H2}} \frac{39.4\ \mathrm{kWh/kg}}{1000\ \mathrm{kWh/MWh}}. \]

The HHV basis enables EUR/MWh-H2-HHV and EUR/kg-H2 reporting. It does not make hydrogen and electricity equivalent end-use services.

Efficiency and Auxiliary Demand

Efficiencies and auxiliary loads are applied in physical component order before annual availability. The owner of each component defines:

  • input and output energy or mass;
  • efficiency equation and operating basis;
  • auxiliary demand and where it is withdrawn;
  • applicable time step; and
  • whether any outage or derating is already embedded.

The result is architecture-specific pre-availability annual energy, \(E_{\mathrm{preavailability},a}\).

Availability Combination

For architecture \(a\):

\[ E_{\mathrm{delivered},a} = E_{\mathrm{preavailability},a} \prod_{s\in S_a} A_s, \]

where \(S_a\) contains only non-overlapping annual subsystem factors.

The rules are:

  1. each outage cause appears once;
  2. serial non-overlapping subsystem factors may be multiplied;
  3. the component owner supplies an already-combined factor for parallel units or redundancy;
  4. a factor is omitted when the effect is embedded in the upstream energy calculation;
  5. an unresolved required factor returns not parameterized for complete delivered energy; and
  6. 100% is permitted only as an explicit zero-outage scenario assumption, not as an evidence-backed reliability result.

Platform availability is not multiplied as a generic extra factor when the same platform-supported equipment is already included in a subsystem boundary. If a future platform factor covers structural or common-utility outages not included elsewhere, its owner must state that non-overlapping scope before it is added.

Legacy Availability Values

Earlier documentation listed flat values between 98% and 100%, including conflicting 98.5% and 99.5% electrolyser-related values. Their subsystem boundaries and evidence are not sufficient to select one as the adopted factor.

Those values are therefore not active architecture-ledger inputs. Component owners may retain them as clearly labelled legacy assumptions or sensitivities until they are evidenced and scoped. In particular, 100% pipeline availability is only a zero-outage scenario, not a reliability conclusion.
