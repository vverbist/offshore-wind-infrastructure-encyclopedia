Agreed Reference Case: IJmuiden Ver Beta

Assumptions, reasoning and evidence for O1

Purpose and status

Agreed on 23 September 2026. This case provides the common wind-farm and onshore hydrogen-delivery basis for the centralised and turbine-level comparisons. It is an idealised case based on IJmuiden Ver Beta, not a claim to reproduce the developer’s final design.

The authoritative article inputs are in reference_case.toml. Values displayed here are loaded from that record; dimensions and spacing are calculated by the reference-case calculation. These are case choices, not defaults for the component methodology. Both architecture templates include the same generated common case (see Common operating case); this note does not claim a completed production or cost run. Remaining preparation is tracked in O1.

The installed wind capacity is derived, not independently prescribed:

\[ P_{\mathrm{farm}}=N P_r. \]

Here \(P_{\mathrm{farm}}\) is in MW, \(N\) is a turbine count and \(P_r\) is in MW/turbine. The result is 2010 MW. “Approximately 2 GW” is a descriptive label; calculations retain the exact count-times-rating result without introducing an electricity-grid export cap.

Layout and spacing derived from area

Let \(B\) be crosswind breadth and \(L\) along-wind length, both in km. With \(A\) in km² and dimensionless \(r=B/L\):

\[ B=\sqrt{Ar},\qquad L=\sqrt{A/r},\qquad BL=A. \]

This gives 19.30 km across the wind and 9.65 km along the wind. The adopted regular-grid construction chooses the number of crosswind columns \(n_B\) and along-wind rows \(n_L\) as:

\[ n_B=\left\lceil\sqrt{Nr}\right\rceil,\qquad n_L=\left\lceil N/n_B\right\rceil. \]

The resulting grid has 17 columns and 8 rows: 7 full rows and a centred final row of 15 turbines. This row convention is an explicit modelling choice; area and aspect ratio alone do not uniquely define a turbine layout.

Turbine centres span the equivalent rectangle, with no additional boundary setback. Consequently, the separations in metres are:

\[ d_B=\frac{1000B}{n_B-1},\qquad d_L=\frac{1000L}{n_L-1}. \]

The factor 1000 converts km to m. Crosswind spacing is 1206.4 m and along-wind spacing is 1378.8 m. The shorter final row retains \(d_B\) and is centred within the same breadth. All wake and collection calculations must use the resulting common turbine coordinates, rather than a separate area-based spacing proxy.

Replacing the net site by a rectangle deliberately omits its actual shape, internal corridors and exclusions. This is a comparative layout, not a demonstration of site-permit compliance. Detailed polygon checks remain D7.

Wind record and orientation

Use the supplied DOWA-derived hourly workbook and fitted sector table already described on Wind Resource and Weibull Distribution. Their paths are recorded in the case file. DOWA is the underlying atlas (Royal Netherlands Meteorological Institute (KNMI) 2018); the atlas citation does not independently establish the workbook’s extraction point or height. Retain the page’s existing metadata and period limitations without substituting an unrelated wind record.

The adopted orientation follows the centre of the most frequent direction sector in the supplied table: 225°, interpreted as the meteorological direction from which wind arrives, clockwise from north. Thus the short axis runs southwest–northeast and the long axis northwest–southeast. Sector labels in the existing file are lower bin edges; use bin centres when preparing wake states. The centre is calculated from the stored sector spacing rather than entered as another independent case value.

This documents the adopted interpretation. It does not recover the missing direction-convention metadata for the original extraction. Both architectures must receive identical annual wind-state weights and turbine-level generation before architecture-specific losses.

Common operating case

prepare_common_case.py turns this record into scenarios/common_case.toml and the data files in scenarios/common/. Both architecture templates include that file. The model rejects any key defined in both a template and the included file, so the templates cannot silently depart from the common case. The script requires the optional PyWake dependency; the generated files are committed so that running a case does not.

Coordinates. Positions are in metres with \(x\) east, \(y\) north and the origin at the farm centre. With the dominant direction \(\theta\) (wind from, clockwise from north), the downwind and crosswind unit vectors are

\[ \mathbf{e}_d=(-\sin\theta,\,-\cos\theta),\qquad \mathbf{e}_c=(\cos\theta,\,-\sin\theta). \]

A turbine in row \(i\) at crosswind offset \(u\) is placed at \(u\,\mathbf{e}_c+\left(i-\tfrac{n_L-1}{2}\right)d_L\,\mathbf{e}_d\), with \(u\) centred within its row. Turbines are numbered row by row from the upwind edge. The centralised AC strings follow this order, which is provisional until the platform location is set (O3/O7).

Turbine curve. The tabulated electrical power and thrust coefficient come from the release’s rotor-performance table, pinned by commit and checksum in the record. The table’s power column is electrical output: its power coefficient is the aerodynamic coefficient times the 95.7% generator efficiency. This matches the model’s common generator-output boundary. Solver noise of up to 0.06 kW above rating is capped at the 15 MW rating. Zero power and thrust are declared just outside the 3–25 m/s operating range, because PyWake would otherwise hold the first tabulated value for waked turbines below cut-in; idle-rotor thrust is neglected.

The adopted turbine electrical boundary is now inverter AC output, before the transformer. The source-table description above records the existing data provenance, not a verified conversion to that boundary. TODO (O2): confirm which converter losses the table includes or document its conversion before recovering inverter losses for decentralised hydrogen. Existing operating states have not been relabelled or rescaled.

Operating states. The fitted sector table is converted to one joint table of direction and speed states. Direction is spread uniformly within each 30° sector in 5° steps, and speed is binned in 0.5 m/s steps represented by bin midpoints between cut-in and cut-out. Weights are the Weibull probabilities times the central annual hours; the 521 h/year below cut-in or above cut-out form one zero-power state. This gives 3,169 states, including that state. The hourly speeds are treated as hub-height speeds, because the workbook does not record its extraction height. This is an explicit assumption carried over from the legacy model, not a height correction.

PyWake 2.6.20 evaluates every state with the model’s wake configuration and the central turbulence intensity 5.8%. The resulting table gives wake-affected power for each turbine at the generator output, before any architecture-specific loss. A zero-hour design state, design-all-rated, sets every turbine at rated power as the common peak-duty basis. The annual states reach this full-farm output at high wind speeds, so it is an attained operating condition rather than an added margin.

Export and onshore delivery boundary

The reported product is hydrogen delivered at the common onshore receipt interface at the adopted pressure. Include the export pipeline, landfall and receipt functions needed to reach that boundary; exclude subsequent backbone transport, storage, distribution and end use. The receipt equipment scope and its supply, installation and operating costs remain O9.

The agreed export length is a scenario assumption. For context, the published Beta electricity-connection assessment gives a 146 km offshore cable route toward Maasvlakte (“Net Op Zee IJmuiden Ver Beta” 2021, Table 2-14, p. 67). This is route evidence for a different carrier, not an adopted hydrogen-pipeline length. A particular landfall and engineered route remain unresolved.

The West-Netherlands hydrogen-network document describes initial operation at 30–50 bar and possible future operation up to 66 bar (Concept Notitie Reikwijdte En Detailniveau 2026, 18). Those are source-reported network conditions; the article deliberately adopts the upper level as its delivery requirement. It does not claim that every connection currently requires that pressure.

Compression and pipeline equations use absolute pressure. The agreed delivery and stack outlet pressures are gauge values and are converted once, at the model boundary:

\[ p_{\mathrm{abs}}=p_{\mathrm{g}}+p_{\mathrm{atm}}. \]

All pressures in this equation are in bar. The atmospheric basis \(p_{\mathrm{atm}}\) is the standard atmosphere, 1.01325 bar (exactly 101 325 Pa (Thompson and Taylor 2008)), applied at both the offshore stack outlet and the onshore receipt boundary. This is an explicit modelling assumption: both points are near sea level, and weather-driven barometric variation, of the order of a few hundredths of a bar, is not modelled.

The result is a delivery pressure of 67.01 bar(a) and a stack outlet pressure of 31.01 bar(a), calculated by the reference-case calculation. Scenario inputs take these absolute values; the gauge values are never copied into an absolute-pressure field. Any pressure loss through receipt equipment belongs upstream of the delivery interface and must be accounted for explicitly rather than hidden in a pressure margin.

Stack sizing and comparison cases

Define the dimensionless overplant factor as:

\[ f=\frac{P_{\mathrm{stack,requested}}}{P_{\mathrm{wind,served}}}, \]

where both capacities are in MW at their stated rating boundaries. Central stacks serve the complete farm; each turbine-level package serves its turbine. Actual installed stack capacity follows the model’s integer module sizing. Overplanting changes stack capacity; it does not automatically increase the wind rating or BOP design capacity. Operation must still cover auxiliary loads within available wind power.

Start both architectures at the agreed baseline factor. Retain the agreed upper factor for deferred sensitivity work (D6), not as a demonstrated feasible design or an optimised result. Preserve the article’s centralised, conservative turbine-level and converter-reduced comparison cases; electrical equipment and efficiency choices remain O2.

Pressure and export-diameter design grid

The article design grid, agreed on 1 October 2026, evaluates compressor discharge pressures of 50–150 bar(g) in 10 bar steps and export-pipe internal diameters of 4–8 inches in 0.5 inch steps. These are scenario choices, not supplier qualification limits or a selected baseline. The grid record is authoritative: its 11 pressures and 9 diameters give 99 combinations per architecture.

The design-case runner expands that record. It adds the central standard atmospheric pressure to each gauge pressure and multiplies each diameter in inches by the exact conversion 0.0254 m/inch. The resulting absolute pressures and internal diameters feed the existing compression and pipeline calculations; they are recorded in each run’s design_cases.csv and case results.

The 50 and 60 bar(g) cases cannot supply the agreed 66 bar(g) delivery boundary without intermediate recompression, which is outside this design. They remain in the comparison as infeasible. Higher pressures pass only this initial boundary check; they are not thereby proven feasible. Pipe capacity and integer parallel-pipe count must still be checked against calculated production.

For central production, compressor discharge is the export inlet. For turbine-level production, collection losses intervene: section diameters and inlet/outlet pressures must support a separately declared export inlet pressure. The grid does not impose its export diameter on collection sections or assume zero collection pressure loss. These section choices remain O7. Cases lacking required inputs report not_parameterized, without selecting a baseline or claiming a complete cost comparison. O3 platform sizing still requires the equipment duties, masses and feeder interface.

For example, from the repository root:

uv run python research_articles/turbine_level_hydrogen/analysis/run_design_cases.py research_articles/turbine_level_hydrogen/scenarios/centralised.toml research_articles/turbine_level_hydrogen/scenarios/pressure_diameter_grid.toml --output model_runs/central-pressure-diameter

Use the decentralised template and a separate output directory for the turbine-level case. Each output directory must be new; results remain local.

Remaining preparation

O1 is partially resolved. The site, count/rating, area, schematic geometry, representative depth, wind-data basis, export distance, delivery-pressure and stack-outlet pressure interpretations and overplanting range are agreed. Before claiming executable common-case results:

  • The common coordinates, turbine definition, operating and peak-duty states, and converted pressures are connected to both templates (above).
  • Complete the remaining design inputs needed by the selected branches: collection section pressures/diameters and distributed export inlet (O7), feeder bays (O3), and electrical interface inputs (O2). Collection origins and network design are linked to O3/O7; onshore receipt details remain O9.
