Platform Material CAPEX

Role in the Model

This page owns the material and fabrication CAPEX of a central offshore platform. The calculation is common to the electricity-export and centralised-hydrogen architectures: architecture-specific equipment determines the hosted mass, and this page converts that physical requirement into the platform structure, its support structure and their cost.

The calculation is a mass chain. Hosted-equipment mass sets the topside mass; the topside mass sets the jacket and pile masses; each mass is multiplied by a unit cost for its own scope. The same masses are passed to Platform and Substation Installation for lift screening.

Equipment cost and equipment mass have different owners. The component pages book the equipment CAPEX and provide the equipment mass required for platform sizing. This page uses the equipment mass only to size the structure and to cost yard integration; it never applies a structural fabrication rate to equipment mass. Onshore yard integration covers placing, fixing, connecting and testing the hosted equipment in the topside before sail-away; offshore hook-up and commissioning remain with the installation page.

The architecture pages determine whether a central platform is present. The relevant component pages determine the hosted-equipment inventory and mass. This page receives those results; it does not recreate architecture-specific equipment models.

\(k_{\mathrm{str}}\) represents the deck steel, primary and secondary structure, modules, buildings, cladding and structural outfitting that surround the hosted equipment. It must be sourced from published topside dry-weight breakdowns that separate equipment from structure; no such breakdown is adopted yet.

\(k_J\), \(a_P\) and \(b_P\) are the substructure and pile relationships in ORBIT’s offshore-substation design module, where the substructure mass is \(0.4\) times the complete topside mass and the pile mass is \(8\,m_J^{0.5574}\) with both masses in tonnes (National Renewable Energy Laboratory 2026). ORBIT documents them as curve fits without identifying the underlying data set (National Renewable Energy Laboratory 2026). They are adopted as top-down relationships because they are the only public mass chain found that links topside mass to jacket and pile mass. Their implicit basis is a crane-installed, piled-jacket HVAC substation; application to HVDC and electrolysis topsides is an extrapolation (see Limitations).

Piles are open-ended fabricated tubular steel, which is the same product class as a monopile. The monopile fabrication unit cost is therefore reused for piles. The monopile page’s transition-piece allowance is not applied, because a pile has no transition piece.

Yard integration is costed per tonne of hosted equipment. This linear form is a modelling assumption: integration work is expected to grow with the quantity of equipment installed, but no public evidence has been found to support a specific relationship. The line is kept separate so that it can be recalibrated or replaced when a supplier breakdown is available.

Evidence Value Use on this page

No public source was found that gives hosted-equipment masses for MW-scale PEM stacks, electrolyser balance of plant, rectifier transformers or hydrogen compressors on an offshore platform, or a topside dry-weight breakdown separating equipment from structure. Design announcements for offshore electrolysis platforms (for example 100–800 MW concepts) report capacity and volume but not mass. The ABB converter catalogue’s 6.2 t for a 10 MW wind converter is recorded on Power Conversion Equipment as context only.

Proposed multiplicity rule (not adopted). Choose the smallest \(N_{\mathrm{plat}}\) and, for each platform, the smallest \(N_{\mathrm{mod}}\) such that \(m_{S,\max}\) and \(m_J\) each stay within the selected installation class. This keeps \(N_{\mathrm{plat}}\) and \(N_{\mathrm{mod}}\) explicit scenario inputs checked against documented lift limits, rather than a capacity rule of thumb. It cannot be applied until \(m_{\mathrm{eq}}\) and \(k_{\mathrm{str}}\) exist; the page’s single-platform example remains illustrative.

Calculation

The calculation is performed per platform and multiplied by \(N_{\mathrm{plat}}\) at the cost step.

Step 1: hosted-equipment mass.

\[ m_{\mathrm{eq}}=\sum_k m_{\mathrm{eq},k} \]

Step 2: topside mass.

\[ m_{\mathrm{str}}=k_{\mathrm{str}}\,m_{\mathrm{eq}}, \qquad m_{\mathrm{top}}=m_{\mathrm{eq}}+m_{\mathrm{str}} \]

\(m_{\mathrm{top}}\) is the complete topside dry mass in tonnes. It is the quantity that \(k_J\) is referenced to.

Step 3: topside lift modules.

\[ N_S=N_{\mathrm{plat}}N_{\mathrm{mod}}, \qquad m_{S,\max}=\frac{m_{\mathrm{top}}}{N_{\mathrm{mod}}} \]

The topside is assumed to be split into modules of equal mass. \(N_{\mathrm{mod}}\) is a discrete input, so a split topside is represented as a step in lift count rather than a smooth mass correction.

Step 4: jacket and pile masses.

\[ m_J=k_J\,m_{\mathrm{top}}, \qquad m_P=a_P\,m_J^{\,b_P} \]

with \(m_J\) and \(m_P\) in tonnes. \(a_P\) carries the dimension \(\mathrm{t}^{1-b_P}\) implied by the source fit.

Step 5: material and fabrication CAPEX.

\[ C_{\mathrm{str}}=c_{\mathrm{str}}\,m_{\mathrm{str}}, \qquad C_{\mathrm{int}}=c_{\mathrm{int}}\,m_{\mathrm{eq}}, \qquad C_J=c_J\,m_J, \qquad C_P=\frac{c_P\,e_{2022}}{X}\,m_P \]

\[ C_{\mathrm{plat}}=N_{\mathrm{plat}}\left(C_{\mathrm{str}}+C_{\mathrm{int}}+C_J+C_P\right) \]

All terms are in EUR2025. \(C_P\) is escalated in USD before conversion, following the common price basis.

Step 6: averaged specific cost.

\[ c_{\mathrm{plat}}=\frac{C_{\mathrm{plat}}}{P_{\mathrm{plat}}} \]

\(c_{\mathrm{plat}}\) in EUR2025/kW is a reporting and comparison metric only. The model uses \(C_{\mathrm{plat}}\), which responds to mass, not to rated power.

If any input is unresolved, the corresponding mass or cost is reported as not parameterised rather than zero.

Two platforms are compared on the same power basis:

  • HVDC converter platform (electricity export). A 2 GW AC-collection and HVDC-converter platform, matching the TenneT 2 GW reference system (TenneT 2024).

Interface with Installation

This page provides the physical platform definition used by the installation model: \(N_{\mathrm{plat}}\), \(N_S\), \(m_J\), \(m_P\) and \(m_{S,\max}\). The installation page independently checks transport and lift feasibility and calculates vessel occupation and offshore installation CAPEX.

A material-CAPEX relationship cannot be used as an installation-cost proxy. Likewise, installation vessel selection or duration must not be embedded in the material cost calculated here.

Limitations

Limitation Consequence
\(k_J\), \(a_P\) and \(b_P\) are undocumented curve fits for HVAC substations Jacket and pile mass for large HVDC and electrolysis topsides are extrapolations; their uncertainty is not quantified
Jacket mass does not depend on water depth, soil or environmental loads Depth-driven differences between sites are not captured
Topside mass is proportional to equipment mass Differences in footprint, deck count and layout density between equipment types enter only through \(k_{\mathrm{str}}\)
Equal topside module split \(m_{S,\max}\) is underestimated when modules are unequal
Linear yard-integration cost per tonne of equipment Integration of dense electrical equipment and of process equipment may differ materially per tonne
ORBIT’s topside-mass relationship, \(3.85\) t/MVA of transformer rating plus 285 t (National Renewable Energy Laboratory 2026), is not used It duplicates the equipment-mass ledger and applies only to HVAC transformer platforms
