Platform Material CAPEX

Model Output and Boundary

This module converts complete topside dry mass \(M\) (t) and water depth \(h\) (m) into structural masses and platform supply CAPEX. The centralised architecture has one platform. Equipment purchase remains with the equipment modules; equipment mass remains inside \(M\).

The overall mapping is

\[ C_{\mathrm{platform}}(M,h) =C_{\mathrm{structure}}(M,h)+C_{\mathrm{yard}}+C_{\mathrm{installation}}. \]

All adopted costs must be in EUR2025. This is installed platform infrastructure CAPEX, excluding hosted process/electrical equipment purchase, cables/pipelines, and project-wide engineering, insurance, contingency and financing. It is not an all-in converter-station or electrolysis-plant price.

Cost Owner and boundary
Topside structural fabrication, jacket and piles This page; structural steel, assembly and coatings
Yard integration This page; equipment placement, connections and yard testing, excluding equipment purchase and work already in fabrication
Transport, offshore installation, hook-up and commissioning Platform Installation; added once
Stacks, electrical equipment, BOP, compressors and shared process equipment Component owners; their mass is included in \(M\), their purchase cost is excluded here

Missing costs remain not parameterised. A known structural subtotal is not labelled total platform cost. The executable workflow exposes the component costs, known supply subtotal, supply total and combined platform total separately.

Inputs and Topside Mass

A supplied topside mass must include structure, electrical collection and conversion equipment, stacks, BOP, compressors and shared auxiliaries. Collection switchgear remains in the equipment scope. Counting feeder bays is not required; AC string counts and cable routes are still calculated independently. This simplification removes the bay-capacity check, so it does not establish a buildable switchgear layout.

Where no complete design mass is available, the optional upstream estimator is

\[ M=M_{\mathrm{ref}}\left(\frac{P}{P_{\mathrm{ref}}}\right)^\alpha. \]

\(M_{\mathrm{ref}}\) is a complete reference topside mass (t); \(P\) and \(P_{\mathrm{ref}}\) are consistent package ratings (GW). The rating must cover the same equipment duty and installed stack capacity as the reference. \(\alpha\) is dimensionless, with a linear baseline of 1 (explicit modelling assumption). Sensitivities override that named exponent without changing the baseline. No separate scaling laws for stacks, electrical, BOP, compression and shared equipment are adopted: compatible reference masses are missing.

The available concept evidence is useful but incomplete:

  • North Sea Energy reports 9500 t for a 0.5 GW concept at 45 m, including 5510 t of structure. Its equipment scope excludes dryers, deoxygenators and compressors in section 4.4.1.1; later compressor references make the boundary ambiguous. It is not a complete reference for this model (North Sea Energy 2022, Table 4.9).
  • Iv publishes 11000 t for its associated concept. The different totals must be reconciled by design revision and scope, rather than averaged (Iv, n.d.).

Both are reference evidence only. Scaling either to the article capacity would still require completing the equipment boundary.

Structural Mass Chain

Use the DNV/DEA preliminary relations as a screening method, replacing the former depth-independent ORBIT jacket ratio (DNV 2023, sec. 3.4).

\[ m_S=f_S M,\qquad m_J=a_J h M^{b_J},\qquad m_P=a_P(M+m_J)+b_P. \]

\(m_S\), \(m_J\) and \(m_P\) are topside structural, jacket and pile masses in tonnes. \(M\) already includes \(m_S\): do not add it again. The residual \(M-m_S\) is an aggregate equipment mass, not an equipment inventory.

Applying them to very large topsides is an unvalidated extrapolation; soil, environmental loads, footprint and layout are not resolved.

Structural Cost and Overall Mapping

Let \(c_S\), \(c_J\) and \(c_P\) be fabrication rates in EUR2025/t for their respective structural scopes. Then

\[ C_{\mathrm{structure}}=c_Sm_S+c_Jm_J+c_Pm_P. \]

Substitution gives the reproducible direct relationship:

\[ \boxed{C_{\mathrm{platform}}(M,h)= (f_Sc_S+a_Pc_P)M +a_J(c_J+a_Pc_P)hM^{b_J} +b_Pc_P+C_{\mathrm{yard}}+C_{\mathrm{installation}}.} \]

This exposes the linear topside term, depth-dependent jacket term, pile intercept, yard integration and installation. It does not claim that mass alone determines integration or installation.

The DNV rates cover structural fabrication rather than equipment purchase; their price basis must be established before normalization (DNV 2023, sec. 3.4). They are stored as reference-only inputs and cannot enter the adopted CAPEX calculation. Publication year is not automatically price year. Piles no longer inherit a turbine-foundation fabrication rate.

An aggregate yard cost is retained because a defensible labour/activity breakdown is unavailable. It is an explicit top-down input, not a fabricated EUR/t rate or the residual of a converter contract minus estimated steel. Its equipment scope and project basis must be documented before adoption.

Commercial Cross-Checks

The TenneT, Petrofac, MHB and Dragados HVDC records concern 2 GW blocks; Dunkerque is an HVAC substation. These records have different boundaries. Award dates identify the announcements, not normalized price years. No currency conversion or regression is applied.

Topside mass includes equipment; equipment purchase inclusion is unconfirmed. Offshore installation is not listed. Depth and price basis unresolved.
RTE Dunkerque, December 2024 (RTE 2024) Approximately 320 million EUR Offshore HVAC substation and foundation EPCI; cables separate. Equipment and structural costs are not split, so not a mass-rate anchor.
Dragados HVDC fact sheet (Dragados Offshore 2024) 24000 t topside and 10000 t jacket per block Physical comparator only; no isolated commercial amount or matched site depth established here.

The apparent discrepancy between structural estimates and billion-euro converter packages is therefore mainly a boundary problem. These sources do not justify assigning the difference to platform integration, or transferring an HVDC package price to electrolysis. A mass-cost calibration requires matched scope, site depth, equipment procurement, installation inclusions, currency and price year.

Installation Handoff and Remaining Inputs

The platform module returns \(M\), \(m_S\), \(m_J\) and \(m_P\) independently of lift design. One platform can require multiple lifts. The installation module uses an explicitly selected integer module count and an equal-module screening assumption; a failed crane-capacity screen blocks that method rather than creating more platforms. A float-over design requires another installation branch.
