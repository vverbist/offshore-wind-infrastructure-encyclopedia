Complete Topside Mass

Calculation and Boundary

This page estimates the complete topside mass of one central electrolysis or offshore HVDC platform. The result drives Platform EPCI CAPEX. Complete topside includes hosted equipment, auxiliaries and topside structure. Jacket and piles are excluded from this mass, while their delivery remains within the EPCI cost boundary.

We use published aggregate masses because the available evidence does not support consistent component-level mass fits. Stack, BOP, compressor and electrical purchase costs retain their component owners; their masses are not added again to the complete topside estimate. No structural fraction is required.

Central electrolysis 0.5 GW DC

13000 t

 OffsH2ore concept: rough estimate including equipment and all steel, based on equipment information and engineering experience (Fraunhofer ISE and project partners 2023, 13–15 and 48).
Offshore HVDC 2 GW transmission rating

30000 t

 IJmuiden Ver Alpha project disclosure; jacket is separately reported as 10000 t (Malaysia Marine and Heavy Engineering Holdings Berhad 2023).

These are concept/project estimates, not measured as-built weights. OffsH2ore includes process equipment, electrical conversion, purification, compression and utilities. Its high-pressure ship-export duty differs from the pipeline-export architecture evaluated here. Using its complete mass without a duty correction is an explicit simplification; the evidence does not support a reliable subtraction and replacement of individual equipment masses.

The NSE/Iv concept is a scope check, not an additional fitted point: the NSE report gives 9500 t at 0.5 GW, but its equipment boundary excludes dryers, deoxygenators and compressors (North Sea Energy 2022, sec. 4.4.1.1 and table 4.9). Iv’s project page reports 11000 t for the concept (Iv, n.d.). These versions must not be treated as independent, scope-matched complete-plant observations.

Capacity to Mass

For the selected architecture,

\[ M_{\mathrm{top}}=M_{\mathrm{top,ref}} \left(\frac{P}{P_{\mathrm{ref}}}\right)^\alpha. \]

\(M_{\mathrm{top}}\) and \(M_{\mathrm{top,ref}}\) are complete topside masses in tonnes; \(P\) and \(P_{\mathrm{ref}}\) are ratings in GW on the same boundary. For electrolysis, use installed electrolyser DC nameplate capacity, including installed overplanting, rather than average operating power or wind-farm nameplate. For HVDC, use station transmission rating. Neither denominator is a compressor motor rating.

The dimensionless exponent \(\alpha=b58fc729-690b-4000-b19f-365a4093b2ff;7B7B3C2076617220706C6174666F726D2D6D6173732D7363616C696E672D6578706F6E656E74203E7D7D;\) is an adopted linear modelling assumption, not a regression. A single reference per architecture cannot identify a fitted exponent. The implied intensities are 26 t/MW DC for electrolysis and 15 t/MW for HVDC, derived by dividing each reference mass by its rating.

The fixed inputs live in model_data/inputs.csv: platform-offsh2ore-topside, platform-offsh2ore-power, platform-mhb-topside, platform-mhb-power and platform-mass-scaling-exponent. The shared calculation is estimate_topside_mass in model/platforms/platform_material_capex.py; the EPCI function consumes its result directly.

Scenario Interface and Limits

Supply topside_mass_t directly, or select mass_method = "power_scaling" and mass_reference = "electrolysis". In the central hydrogen workflow, installed DC nameplate follows the existing stack-sizing calculation:

\[ P_{\mathrm{ELX,DC}}= \left\lceil\frac{f_{\mathrm{overplant}}P_{\mathrm{wind}}}{P_{\mathrm{module}}}\right\rceil P_{\mathrm{module}}. \]

All powers in this equation are in kW. \(P_{\mathrm{wind}}\) is turbine count times turbine rating; \(f_{\mathrm{overplant}}\) is the scenario’s dimensionless requested stack-capacity ratio; \(P_{\mathrm{module}}\) is stack-module-rating from the shared input table. The ceiling rounds up to whole modules. Divide the resulting kW by \(10^6\) before using the GW-based mass equation. Stack purchase, operation and platform mass share this sizing rule. It does not require operating efficiency, wind states or a separately entered DC rating. A separate rated_power_gw is rejected for this branch to prevent inconsistent sizing.

The "hvdc" and "custom" branches retain explicit rated_power_gw. A custom reference also requires reference mass and rating inputs. Direct and scaled masses cannot be supplied together.

The agreed simplification keeps one platform even when extrapolating to GW-scale electrolysis. It does not establish a feasible deck layout, support structure, transport or lift plan. Changes in process duty, equipment density, foundation class or installation method can invalidate smooth scaling. Applying the HVDC-derived EPCI coefficient to electrolysis remains a separate transfer assumption.

Remaining evidence: a matched commercial electrolysis-platform mass and EPCI scope, and independent masses at additional ratings. Until available, sensitivity to reference mass, the mass exponent and the EPCI coefficient should be recorded explicitly; component-level fits and a statistical confidence interval are not justified.
